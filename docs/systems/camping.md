# Camping (roadmap 2.7)

How the party camps from the World tab's Camp ([ui](ui.md#the-world-tab)): the check, the ambush, the half heal, the camp scene and the eight hours. From 1.0's machine code (`campMenuController` at `0x80041020`, `Party::CampAmbushCheck` `0x80078d14`, `Camping.Ambushed` `0x800788cc`, `Party::CampHeal` `0x80078ba4`, `TrekLoop.CampScene` `0x80004b0c`, `TrekLoop.CampsiteScene` `0x80004cd4`, `World::Lapse8Hours` `0x8007c098`) and the ROM plugin's records.

## Where

- **The only check is the terrain** of the chunk the player is in: its camp factor must be above 0 (`CodeTable.CampFactors`, `0x800e2f38`, 30 floats). Nothing else is checked: not towns, enemies, the map set or the time. Refused, the menu stays open and says "The party cannot camp here.", a literal in the code, in the Party tab's message box.
- **The factors:** 20 for terrains 0, 3, 4 and 27; 15 for 1 and 24; 10 for 2, 5, 7 and 17; 50 for 10; 30 for 20, 21 and 22; 0 for the rest. So 164 of the Overworld's 172 chunks allow camping (terrains 19 and 23 don't), and no other map set does, but EhudUnder's C09 (terrain 4) and MiscInteriors' I05 (terrain 2).
- **Combat's pause menu** has no World tab, so there's no camping in combat.

## What happens

`Party::CampAmbushCheck`, as the menu closes:

1. **The ambush roll**, once per camp. Only while derived flag 5004 holds (two of Abrecan, Godric and Rheda have joined; flags 89–91). The member best at Ranger (`GetMostSkilledMember`: the four slots in order, dead or not, the later on a tie) gives r, their modified Ranger, 0 if it's 0 or less and at most 10. The party is ambushed if d100 ≤ trunc((15 − r) × 1/15 × the factor), in single precision: the factor in percent with no Ranger, a third of it at 10. The 1/15 is 0.0666667 (`0x800d3814`). Ambushed, nothing else happens: no heal and no time.
2. **Otherwise the half heal** (`CampHeal` with half set, the table `{1.0, 0.5}` at `0x800d386c`): each living member regains half their maximum hit points, truncated, up to what's missing, and 24,000 ticks of play pass for their effects (8 hours at the clock's 72: `Entity::IncEffects`), so a potion with 8 hours or less left runs out. Then every battery, worn or in the party's inventory, refills by half its capacity. The loop covers every stack. Nothing is eaten, and no skill or attribute changes the amount. The camping scripts have no `CampHeal` op, so camping heals half, not fully; only nine conversations heal fully.
3. **The camp scene**: flag 4240 is set while the game looks through the map objects of the chunk the player is in (`gameVars.borg9DatPointer`), and the first dialogue object whose flag A is one of eight (the list `CodeTable.CampDialogueFlags` at `0x800d79f0`) and holds starts its script, as using it would. Distance doesn't count: the dialogue object's trigger check is called without it (`0x800113cc`). With none, the Campsite's map (`aidyn:map/0x185C`, MiscInteriors C05) gives its first dialogue object's script, `aidyn:dialogue/0x185B`: Alaron alone at the fire, saying "Ahhhhhh. Pass the marshmallows.". 4240 is cleared again.
4. **Eight hours pass when the scene ends**, as after any script marked `rest` ([scripts](scripts.md)): the clock moves on and the calendar's flags are written (`Lapse8Hours`). Time played doesn't.

## The ambush's enemies

From the camp encounters table ([db](../formats/db.md), ROM `0x1FEDE50`, 80 bytes a terrain: `CampEncounters.Load`, `0x800096DC`): the terrain's row, at the tier of the party's average **modified** Level, dead members counted (`Party.AverageLevel`, `0x80075134`: 5 or less, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20, over 20), two groups of an enemy with a minimum and maximum; the five slots after them are never read. They're made into a monster party's with its least the two minimums summed and its most the two maximums, and rolled as one is (`BattleSetup.FillEnemies`, with the level trim and the Skeleton for none). The arena is the terrain's; the collision byte 2 (an ambush), the animation byte 0, no fleeing, no bonus loot and no encounter flag, so a win sets nothing and remembers nothing. Terrains with no camping have rows of Chaos Spellweavers alone.

**Terrain 27** (six Overworld chunks, N25, O24–O26, P24 and P25, camp factor 20) allows camping but has no row: the original reads the String/Cheats table's ciphertext after it (`0x1FEE6C0`), in which none of the ten IDs is an entity, so `Combat.IsExcludedEnemy` drops them all and the battle has no enemy. What that does in play (a win at once, or a crash) isn't settled ([user checks](../user-checks.md#combat)). Until it is, the port says "The party is ambushed, but no one comes." and goes back to trek, with no battle, no heal and no time (`GameSession.BeginBattle`).

## In the port

- **The records:** each terrain's factor is its `camp` (`aidyn:terrain/n`), and the rest is the camping record (`aidyn:camping/0`): the refusal's text, the ambush's flag and numbers, the heal's share and effect time, the check flag, the eight scenes' flags and the Campsite's map. The importer reads the factors and the scenes' flags from the ROM and carries the rest as facts ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).
- **The rules:** the World tab checks the terrain (`WorldMenu.Accept`), and `TrekLoop.BeginCamp` rolls the ambush (`Camping`, on the world's random stream); then the camp screen opens ([below](#the-camp-screen)), and its Rest heals (`TrekLoop.CampRest`, `Party.CampHeal`, which a script's camp heal also uses, fully) and cues the night's scene, the story's as a dialogue object's cut-scene. The hours pass as the scene ends (`GameState.Lapse`), for any script that ends with rest hours: a camp scene, or a conversation's camp heal; the camp's other hours pass at once as the party rests (`Transition.Rest`).
- **The party sleeps through the camp's hours**, the remade game's (the user's design, Nov 5, 2026; [characters](characters.md#sleep)): `Party.CampHeal` puts every living member's time since sleeping to nothing after their effects age, so Tired and Overtired go, from the World tab's camp and a script's camp heal alike. It doesn't feed them.
- **The food spoils through the camp's hours**, the remade game's (the user's design, Nov 5, 2026; [items](items.md#cooking-and-food)): `Party.CampHeal` ages the inventory's food by its effect ticks, the eight hours, and returns what spoiled and left, which `TrekLoop.Camp` and a script's camp heal note with the game (`GameState.NoteTossed`); when trek shows again after the camp scene, it says so as a toast, "Spoiled and tossed: 2 Meat Stew, 1 Fish." (`Transition.Say`), so nothing leaves the inventory unseen.
- **The ambush** starts a battle (`BattleSetup.CampAmbush`: where the party camped, its terrain's ambush at the party's tier, no fleeing), which trek fades out into: no heal and no time, as in the original. The camp encounters are each terrain's `Ambushes`, five tiers of the two kinds, and the tiers' levels are the camping record's `AmbushLevels`; terrain 27 has none ([above](#the-ambushs-enemies)).
- **The way in** is the pause menu's exit (`PauseExit.Camp`), which `GameSession` carries out once the menu has rolled up, as the original's trek state 0x11 runs its camp (`TrekLoop.CampStart`, `0x80004ac0`).

## The camp screen

The remade game's camp, the user's design (Nov 7, 2026): camping is a screen, not a cut-scene. The party sits round the fire in a painted campsite, does what only a camp allows before it sleeps, and rests for as long as the evening took. It's where the party talks most. The original's camp is kept under it: the terrain check, the ambush roll, the half heal, the eight story scenes and the Campsite's marshmallows are all still there, in their places. The campsite brief (the owner's, outside the repository) says what the picture, the figures and the slots are to be; this is what's built of it, the data and the rules, with the views to come.

**The flow.** Camp on the World tab → `TrekLoop.BeginCamp`: refused or ambushed as before, else `GameSession.OpenCamp` opens the screen (`GameMode.Camp`, `Transition.OpenCamp`; the world is held under it as under the pause menu) on the party's talk, a camp talk (`CampTalks`, `TrekLoop.ShowCampTalk`) in the conversation box over the picture, its done flag set, the user's design (Nov 7, 2026); as it ends, or at once with none to tell, the picture pans up to its foot and the band unrolls there (`Ui.CampMenu`, below) with its five tabs down the left, the user's design (Nov 7, 2026): Camp, each member's name, level, portrait, hit points bar and statuses, with the location, the inventory and Rest along the foot; Food, the Cook's recipes; Potions, the Alchemist's; Weapons, nothing yet; and Armor, the Mechanic's. The cursor is on the tabs or in the tab's content (`CampMenu.Focus`, the user's design, Nov 7, 2026): on the tabs, up and down change the tab and right or A enters the content; in the content, up and down move over a tab's recipes, A makes the cursor's recipe or on Camp rests, and left or B goes back to the tabs; the triggers change the tab from anywhere, C-left and C-right change who makes it among those with the skill, B on the tabs breaks camp, and Start opens the pause menu over the camp, its World tab's Camp dimmed and refusing, "The party is already camped." (`WorldMenu.Camping`), since the party is camped. Rest and Break camp roll the band up first and are carried out as it has closed. Rest → `GameSession.CampRest`: `TrekLoop.CampRest` heals, runs the effects down by the camping record's ticks scaled to the camp's hours, sleeps the party and ages the food, then finds the night's scene, the first of: the story's camp scene for the chunk, cued as its cut-scene (the screen closes as trek fades out, and the scene's eight hours pass as it ends); the Campsite's own script; or nothing. The camp talk was told as the camp opened. The hours the scene won't pass itself pass at once (`CampRestResult.HoursNow`, `Transition.Rest`). B at the top breaks camp with nothing done (`CampChoice.Leave`): no heal, no hours. The ambush stays before the screen, as the original rolls it on trying to camp.

**The hours.** The rest is the camping record's `restHours`, 8, plus each thing made times its activity's `hours`: three dishes at an hour each is an eleven-hour night. `CampMenu.Hours` shows on the Rest row, "Rest (11 hours)".

**The records**, the ACRE plugin's, in `acre:` (`Plugins/Records/CampRecords.cs`; a plain ROM has none, and the screen then offers Rest alone over nothing):

| Record | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `acre:campactivity/<name>` | Something to do before resting: a skill (`skill`, the Alchemist's `aidyn:skill/0`, the Mechanic's `aidyn:skill/4`, the Cook's `acre:skill/12`), whose recipes the screen offers as the Party tab's popups do (`Crafting.Offered`), done by the living member best at it, C-left and C-right choosing another who has it; `hours`, what each thing made adds; `order`. |
| `acre:campsite/<biome>` | A biome's picture (`image`, `width`, `height`), its `fire`, `pot` and `safeUi` as shares of the picture, 0 to 1; its `slots` (a ground anchor `x`, `y`, a perspective `scale`, a draw `depth`, the `poses` it takes, the `facing` a figure has there, whether a mirrored image may face it, `mirror`, and a tend-fire `interaction` point); its `layouts`, each a party `size` and the slots it uses; the `terrains` it's shown on (none is the fallback, `CampsiteRecord.For`); an `ambient` tint; and `calibrated`, false while the anchors are placeholders. Every biome keeps the forest master's camera, fire and ground plane, so one library of figures sits at all of them. |
| `acre:campfigure/<name>` | A character's figure: `entity`, `stature` against the one canonical height, and its `poses`, each an image with its ground-contact `anchorX`, `anchorY`, the `facing` it was painted in, `mirror` where it may be flipped (not with asymmetric gear), an optional `normal` or `lightMask`, and the hands' `handX`, `handY` for tending the fire. The seven canonical poses are `CampPoses`: `stand_profile`, `sit_profile`, `stand_rear_3q`, `sit_rear_3q`, `stand_rear`, `sit_rear`, `tend_fire`. A figure declares what it has; a character with none is placed with nothing to draw. |
| `acre:camptalk/<name>` | A conversation for the night: its `script` (a Conversation), and when: `members` all in the party, `anyone` of these in it, `flags` holding, `terrains`, a `done` flag set once told (none tells it again), and a `weight`. |

**The arrangement** (`Camp/CampArranger`, the brief's sections 4, 5 and 11): the camp's seed is a stable FNV-1a over the world day, the chunk and the sorted party (`CampSeed`), so reopening the camp the same day shows the same arrangement and a new day may show another, nothing depending on the runtime's own hashing. For the party's size the campsite's layouts are tried from where the seed says, yesterday's last if there's another (`avoid`); the members are shuffled by the seed and each put at a free slot of the layout, backtracking, in a pose the slot takes and the figure has, facing the slot's way as painted or mirrored where both allow; the pose among those is the seed's choice. Each member is placed once, nothing is hidden or stretched. A member with no figure is placed with nothing to draw; a party no layout takes, or a size with no layout, gets the known-safe fallback, the first layout or the furthest slots in order; each with a diagnostic (`CampArrangement.Diagnostics`), which `OpenCamp` logs. The placements are drawn furthest first (`InDrawOrder`, by the slot's depth then its height), at the slot's scale times the figure's stature (`CampPlacement.Scale`). `CampTests` holds the cases.

**The talk** (`Camp/CampTalks`): among the talks whose conditions hold, one by weight on the seed, in ID order, so the same night asks the same question, told as the camp opens. The story's eight scenes come with the rest, as the original looks for them; the Campsite's marshmallows last.

**The view** (`CampLayer`, `CampView` in the runtime; the brief's phase 1): a canvas layer under trek's UI, so the box draws the night's talk over it and the cut-scene fade darkens it as a story scene is cued, and over the world, which it covers: the picture as wide as the screen, never squeezed, and where that leaves it taller than the screen, panned by the core's `CampMenu.Pan`: the fire at the screen's centre while the talk shows, then up over a second (`CampMenu.PanTicks`) until the picture's foot meets the screen's, and back down as a talk comes again; a picture that fits is drawn whole, centred; each placement's image at its slot, scaled by the picture's scale on the screen times the slot's and the stature, its anchor on the slot, mirrored by a negative width, tinted by the biome's `ambient`; nothing where a member has no figure yet, and with the overlay a dot on its anchor with its name and pose.

**The band** is the campsite's `safeUi`, the user's layout (Nov 7, 2026; moved down and narrowed from the dialogue box's width the same day): a strip of the pause menu's parchment (`acre:image/parchment`) between two rollers of the pause menu's 3D scroll, the same actor drawn smaller (`PauseScrollView.Lines`, the band's height over the parchment's 228/240 of the scroll's length) with its rollers' columns scaled out to the band's ends (`PauseScrollView.OpenHalfWidth`, against where the hold leaves them, 0.718 of the length), so the two still rise together from the middle and meet there rolled up. It plays the Party tab's opening and closing, the user's choice (Nov 7, 2026): once the pan is up, the band's scroll (`CampMenu.Scroll`, a `PauseScroll` on the Party tab that holds as barter's does and skips the darkening) rises from below and unrolls, the rollers starting together as the actor has them (their way out past the start alone is scaled to the band's ends, `PauseScrollView.StartSpan`) and fading in from nothing as they stand up, opaque by the time they stand, unlike the pause menu's (the user's choice, Nov 7, 2026), the parchment fading in with them and cut between them (`CampView.Band`, a control cut to the rollers); the band is drawn invisible for two frames as the camp opens, so what it draws with loads then and not as it unrolls, where the loading stalled the scroll; Rest, Break camp and the debug button's talk roll it up to the middle and drop it away first, and are carried out as it has closed (`CampMenu.Step`, `CampTick.Closed`, `GameSession.StepCamp`); the session steps it by the camp layer's actor clock (`GameSession.CampScrollClock`) and cues its steps to the actor (`CampScrollCue`), as the pause menu's. Only the open band takes presses (`CampMenu.Open`). On the band, the five tabs down the left, spread from the band's top to its foot, each on a slip of the gold's parchment (`parchment_512x220`) with the user's icon (`ui_camp`, `ui_food`, `ui_potions`, `ui_weapons`, `ui_armor`) and its caption, the others in brown on slips a shade darker behind the content's paper, the wide sheet (`parchment_1800x800`), and the one showing in the cursor's red on a plain slip over it, cut at the paper's edge and joined to it as the Party tab's tab is to its page, sideways (`CampView.JoinTab`), with the pointer before it while the cursor is on the tabs; and on the paper the tab's content, kept clear of the rollers, its own cursor's pointer showing while the cursor is in the content. The mouse over a tab shows it, over a recipe points at it, and a left click on a recipe or Rest presses A, a right click B.

**The Camp tab**, the user's design (Nov 7, 2026): each member's name and level ("Lv 24") in a row over their portrait, 30 square, which pulses amber or red with hunger or tiredness as the Party tab's does (`StatusPulse`); under it the Party tab's hit points bar in its own art (`UiPainter.HealthBar`, which the Party tab draws with too), 7 tall for the hit points on it, "58 / 83", and split into Stamina's lighter red and Endurance's darker by the Party tab's rule (`HeaderNumbers.BarSplits`); under that the statuses stacked in a column, the one place they are rather than icons in a row, each in a badge as wide as the portrait and 6 tall on the tooltip's parchment, its icon fixed at the left, drawn whole so its round edge isn't cut, and its name, Hungry, Starving, Tired or Overtired, centred in the rest (`CampMenu.Badges`); the portraits 2 apart and the level in the fine size, the smallest rung, and Rest in the small; and along the foot, "Current Location: Oisin Forest" (the place naming the chunk, [places](places.md), else the campsite's name, `CampMenu.Location`) and "Total Inventory: 547" (every stack's count, as the Inventory tab's foot says it) at the left, and Rest on a slip at the right with the pointer, the one thing A does here. A recipes tab shows the maker's note and as many recipes as fit with the cursor's among them, a mark at the right where more are above or below, and the last thing said at the foot.

**Debug.** The overlay, off until the Debug button beside Talk or F3 turns it on, and hidden while a talk shows: the fire's cross, the pot, the band's green frame, each slot's anchor with its name, scale and depth, the members' names and poses on their anchors, the seed and the layout, the placeholders' warning, the arrangement's diagnostics, and the rule-of-thirds lines the user drew over the whole screen (`acre:image/debug_thirds`); none of it is the game's.
