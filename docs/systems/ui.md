# UI: text, the conversation box, the pause menu, the title screen, the HUD and the battle screen (roadmap 1F, M2, M3)

How the port lays out and draws the original's UI. The fonts and string tables are in [text](../formats/text.md), the menus' sounds in [audio](audio.md#sound-effects), and the runtime's controls in [runtime](runtime.md). Positions are in the original's 320 × 240 frame, and addresses are 1.0's.

The menus' layouts come from the machine code and the user's captures from the emulator (`roms/menu-*.png`, `hud-*.png`, `pause-*.png`; see the [reference rig](../reference-rig.md#emulator-captures)), mostly from the "Before final encounter" checkpoint. Compare against those captures in the console's own pixels (`--ui-screenshot`), where the port's classic layouts match within the noise of the console's 16-bit dither, about 6 a channel.

## The screen

The original draws its UI on a 320 × 240 screen; the Expansion Pak's high-resolution mode is 512 wide, and `borg8DlistInit` scales images to it. Every UI image is a texture rectangle with bilinear filtering and a combiner of texel × primitive colour (`N64BorgImageDraw`), so text is its sheet's texels times the text's colour, alpha too. At 1:1 the filter samples each texel exactly, and only scaled images are filtered.

- **The soft look is the video output.** The emulator's captures are 640 × 240: each line's 320 pixels spread over 640 by linear interpolation, lines untouched. A real console's VI adds anti-aliasing and divot filters, and the TV more.
- **Overscan bars** (`Graphics::EndList`): in 1.0 and 1.1, black rectangles 16 pixels at the left and right and 12 at the top and bottom, drawn after everything; the EU version draws 18 and 12. The UI's own boxes run under them.

`UiScreen` (in `AidynRetold.Views`, which the runtime and the editor share) draws the UI the same way: views draw in a `SubViewport`, in its pixels, and the screen fills the window by one of three filters (`UiFilter`): **Pixels** (Sharp), **Smooth** (bilinear) and **VideoOut** (each line doubled in width with the averages between, then scaled up sharp, as the emulator's captures show the original). The game is always Sharp, the user's choice (Nov 2, 2026), and the options have no Menu Filter; the editor keeps all three, for setting beside the console's captures. Under Smooth and Video Out the canvas filters linearly and is drawn at its own size, then scaled up. **Sharp is drawn at the window's resolution**, the user's choice (Oct 31, 2026), as the soft look read as blurry beside ReChronicled's: the viewport is the least whole multiple of the screen that fills the window's height (`UiScreen.Oversampling`, 6 for 240 lines on 1440), its 2D stretched from the screen's size (`Size2DOverride`), and shrunk smoothly to the window when it's larger. In it an image drawn at its own size, text among them, is magnified pixel for pixel (the canvas's nearest filter), so it shows exactly the pixels the original's would, square and hard-edged; and one drawn scaled, such as a feathered panel's edges or a scaled HUD sprite, is filtered linearly at that resolution (`UiPainter.Filtered`, a `CanvasTexture` over the image), as the console filters it but without its blur. The pause menu's scroll renders its rollers that much finer too (`PauseScrollView`). A larger replacement image will show its detail there once the game draws it at the original's size ([replacing graphics](../replacing-graphics.md#whats-planned)). The views still lay out and take the mouse in the screen's pixels, and `--ui-screenshot` saves the screen at its own size. The screen is `Lines` pixels tall, 240 by default, and as wide as the window's shape, never narrower than 4:3. Views lay themselves out by its size; a taller screen draws the UI smaller, with room for more, and text is always drawn pixel for pixel and wraps at the same widths. The canvas blends in on-screen values, premultiplied, so a glyph's alpha and colour multiply as the N64's do.

**Scaled images sample as the RDP does:** its first pixel on the first texel and a step of the scale per pixel, where Godot's linear filter starts half a step in. `UiPainter` offsets the source rectangle to match.

## Laying text out

`TextLayout` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is the font engine's layout, over a font record (`Font::PrintMain`, `GetHeightScaled`, `GetWidthScaled`):

- A character draws its glyph at the pen, as wide as its measured width and a whole cell tall, and moves the pen by that width. A character with no glyph is skipped and takes no room.
- **Text wraps only at spaces.** After a space, if the next word would end past the right edge, the line breaks and the space is dropped. A space at the start of a line takes no room, and a word longer than the line runs past the edge. `\n` breaks the line.
- Lines are the cell's height plus 2 apart: 11 pixels in both fonts.
- Text is drawn only inside its widget's bounds. A glyph across an edge is cut, and one outside is dropped. `UiPainter`'s text and images take a clip box, which crops without moving where text wraps.
- Characters from `0x7F` are the controller buttons, drawn in their own colour with the text's alpha.
- **A text widget has no font of its own.** It draws, and measures itself, in whatever font the printer has set at the time (`Font.Select`, `0x800AA5D4`; `WidgetClipText.Draw`, `Height`). The main font is set but while a container draws or steps its children: the Party tab's lists (`PartyMenu.ListsDraw` `0x8003140C`, `ListsStep` `0x800314CC`), training's trainer (`TrainingMenu.Draw` `0x80032288`), barter's pile (`BarterMenu.Draw` `0x8003D0D8`), the portraits (`PartyMenu.PortraitsDraw` `0x8003B9A0`) and the details' and parchment popups (`MenuPopup.Draw` `0x80034AF0`) set the slim font and the main one back after. So everything those hold draws in the slim font, whatever it was made under, and a text measured as it's built is measured in the main font unless its builder set the slim one first.


### The text's typeface

**The port draws text in a typeface at the screen's resolution, the user's choice** (Oct 31, 2026), as ReChronicled's HD text pack does: Marcellus SC (Astigmatic, SIL Open Font License, `assets/fonts`, carried in the views' assembly), Roman small capitals like the menus' own painted labels. The original's two sheets are bitmaps 12 × 9 a cell, which the sharp UI screen shows as square pixels.

- **The face redraws the sheets' letters, digits and marks** (`TextFace`, in `AidynRetold.Views`): each into a copy of its sheet's cells at 8 texels a pixel (`TextFace.Density`), through Godot's text server, with mipmaps, drawn smoothly. Some characters draw icons in the original's sheets, not themselves: `$` a hash, `{` and `}` buttons, `<`, `>`, `|`, `^`, `_`, `` ` ``, `~` and `\` the skull, heart, infinity and other icons, `"` another mark, and `0x7F` on the controller buttons. Those cells stay the sheet's, drawn pixel for pixel (`FaceSheet.Draws`). `:` and `;` share a cell, as `%` and `&` do; the face draws the first.
- **A glyph is as tall as the sheet's capitals** on the sheet's baseline, measured from its H: 8 pixels standing on row 8 in the main font, 7 on row 7 in the slim. Lowercase is the face's small capitals. Each takes the face's advance plus 0.6 pixels, rounded, at most the cell's 12, with the glyph centred in it, as ReChronicled found best: the sheets' widths are wide and loosely spaced.
- **The core lays text out by the face's widths**, so the conversation box, the journal, a backdrop's pages, captions and the credits wrap and page as the text is drawn. The runtime measures them as it applies the settings and hands them over each tick (`SessionSettings.TextWidths`); `GameState.Fonts` (`TextFonts`) gives every layout the core makes, and the session hands the conversation boxes theirs with their text speed. The editor draws and lays out in the face too. A headless run lays out by the sheets' own widths.
- **A mod gives the text another face** with a `.ttf` asset of the font record: `assets/aidyn/font/0.ttf` for the main font and `1.ttf` for the slim ([replacing graphics](../replacing-graphics.md#fonts)).
- **The menus' text is smaller than the fonts' own size, the user's choice** (Nov 2, 2026): its capitals 4.2 pixels of the frame tall (`UiPainter.StandardText`), the size the Party tab's level was first drawn at, the slim font at 0.6. The fonts' size was the console's, as large as its pixels needed; on a fine screen the smaller text reads better. Both fonts are drawn to the same capitals, so the main font at 0.525 and the slim at 0.6, and their text is one size. A heading is 5.6 (`HeadingText`: the member's name over the Party tab), and a tab's caption 2.94 (`CaptionText`).
  - **Which screens:** the pause menu's three tabs, with the Party tab's barter, training and post-battle windows, C-down's details, the skills' popups and the messages and questions over them, and the System tab's pages, which the title's menu shares. A painter's `TextSize` sets it (`UiPainter`), and the views of those screens set it as they're made. The conversation box, the journal, the HUD, the battle screen, trek's popups, backdrops, captions and the credits keep the fonts' size: the journal's and the conversation box's pages are laid out in the core by the fonts' widths, and making them smaller means laying them out smaller there too.
  - **How:** a font has a size (`UiFont.Scale`, set by `UiFont.Sized` from the face's capitals), and its measures, `Width`, `LineHeight` and `Height`, are at that size, rounded to the frame's pixels, so the layouts that measure text follow it. `UiPainter.Text` lays the text out at the font's own size, within the bounds made larger to match, and draws each glyph shrunk toward where the text starts. It moves it down too (`UiFont.Drop`), so the middle of its capitals is where the full-size text has them: text drawn where a layout put the full-size text sits in the middle of the same row, and the layouts' rows, pitches and columns are as they were. Where a layout centres text by hand, it centres the capitals' middle (`UiFont.Middle`, `Capitals`), and letters set one at a time are placed to a fraction of a pixel (`ExactWidth`, and `Text`'s overload taking a fractional x), as whole pixels spaced the name entry's letters unevenly.
  - **Not done yet:** the screens that keep the fonts' size, and tightening the layouts the smaller text leaves room in: the lists' 13 between rows, the inventory's columns and the stats' values were set for the larger text.

## The conversation box

`DialogueBox` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) places what a conversation shows, as `DiaMenuSub::InitDefault` does, and `ConversationBoxView` draws it in the main font. Backdrops and the help menu draw on a full-screen image instead ([below](#books-and-the-help-menu)). The box is laid out in the 320 × 240 frame, placed at the bottom centre of the UI screen. A on its page is `DialogueBox.Accept` ([scripts](scripts.md#how-it-walks)), which conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all press through.

- **The original's band** is translucent black: image `0x1C4`, a single white texel of alpha 136, drawn black. It runs right across the screen from the box's top to 232 (180 plus the portrait border's height), so the overscan bars cut it at 16, 304 and 228. Above it a 10-pixel gradient fades upward, image `0x1C3` squeezed from 16 pixels.
- **The port floats it**, the user's choice, since a wide screen has no bars to cut it off: the band runs from 16 to 304 and down to 228, feathered on every side from the pieces the original's popups are made of (`WidgetChoiceDia::SetImages`, see `FeatheredPanel`): `0x1C3` above, `0x1C2` below, `0x1C0` left and `0x1C1` right, each fading outward from alpha 136, and the rounded corners `0x1BC`–`0x1BF`.
- **The portrait's border** (`0xDF`, 52 × 52) is at x 20, level with the box's top, and the portrait (40 × 40) sits 6 pixels inside it. A speaker with no portrait gets `0x0125`.
- **The line** is in (200, 200, 200), from x 78 to 294, and ends 2 pixels above what's under it: the band's bottom, or the choices. The box's top is 4 pixels above the line, but never below 180, so a short line sits at y 184, and the box grows upward from five lines.
- **Choices** are in tan (200, 180, 100), from x 88, 4 pixels apart, and end at 228. The cursor's choice pulses toward (50, 45, 25, 63) and back over 24 frames each way, whole steps per channel (`WidgetScrollMenu::Tick`).
- **A line before its own speaker's choices shows alone.** When a menu's first choice has the line's speaker, the line shows first, and A brings up the choices alone under Alaron's portrait. The box shrinks to the choices but never below 180, its four lines (Brenna's recruiting shows it); the portrait and its border stay where the line put them, since `Setup` doesn't move them on that path.

- **The port prints a line as a typewriter does, with a voice, the user's choice** (Oct 30, 2026; `TextReveal`, the Enhancements page's Text Speed and the Audio page's Dialogue volume, `--text-speed` and `--text-voice`). The original shows a line whole and plays no sound for it. Under Slow, Normal or Fast the box prints the line half a glyph, one or two glyphs a tick, in the order `TextLayout.Print` places them, so a word is never half-wrapped; its choices wait, laid out where they'll be so the line doesn't move; and A prints the rest (`DialogueAccept.Revealed`), the next A moving on as always. The session hands the box the rate each tick (`SessionSettings.TextRate`, `DialogueBox.GlyphsPerTick`), so the editor's box and a headless run show lines whole. With the voice on, the box counts a blip for a glyph printed, at most one every 3 ticks, or every 2 for a talkative speaker and every 4 for a reserved one, the record's `voiceCadence` (`TextReveal.BlipSpacing`; Godric chatters, Becan, Brenna and Farris are sparing, the user's choice) (`DialogueBox.TakeBlips`), and the runtime sounds each (`TextVoicePlayer`): 55 ms of a soft square at 660 Hz, made in code, rising in 3 ms and dying away, jittered 4% either way, at half the sound volume, beside the core's mixer. Its pitch is the speaker's (`TextReveal.PitchOf`): a man's base of 0.72 or a woman's of 1.15, the user's choice, by the record's `voice`, which the importer judges from the portrait ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)), or 1 for a creature; times the square root of 1.8 over the body's scaled height, the taller the deeper, kept between 0.7 and 1.5, so Alaron is 0.72 and Brenna about 1.18. A big man's voice, the guards', the brutes' and the giants', is 0.5 whatever his height, as deep as each other, the user's choice. A speaker also has a **tone** (`TextReveal.Tones`, the record's `voiceTone`): what the blip is made of and how it rises and dies, which sets who is speaking where the mood sets how. Plain is the soft square as it was; soldier, the guards' and Abrecan's, is a square with an instant attack and a short ring, clipped and precise; soft, a sine that swells over 9 ms and rings long, and feminine, Brenna's, soft at a quarter of its strength; sultry, Keelin's, a smooth sine swelling over 12 ms, ringing long, a touch breathy, a tenth lower and with a slight lilt; sly, Dougal's and Farris's, short and perky, three fifths square, with every other blip raised 7% and the ones between lowered, a sing-song; grand, Donovan's, big: seven tenths square, an eighth lower and ringing long; old, Godric's and Becan's, plain with a tremble: the pitch wobbling 6% within each blip, seven times a second, and a touch of breath; and bright (square) and hollow (triangle) are tried on the rest of the party for comparing, the user's choice; everyone else is plain, which plays 10 dB over the others since it read as quieter, as old does. A tone can also sit lower or higher than the voice and height give (`Pitch`). A mod names a tone on the record, or several joined with `+`, the first the blip and the rest traits laid over it, their tremble, lilt and breath where stronger and their pitch shift on top (`TextReveal.Layer`), as `soldier+old` would be the soldier's clipped blip with the old tremble. The blips play at about −22 dB of the sound volume (`TextVoicePlayer.Gain`, 0.08), the user's choice after a half and a quarter still read as loud. A mod patches a speaker's `voice`, or sets its pitch outright with `voicePitch`, on its entity or dialogue entity record. The same line shown again by a refresh goes on printing where it was. Backdrops and the help menu, which draw on a picture, show their pages whole as before.
- **A line is said in a mood** (`TextMood`, `TextReveal.Presets`, Oct 30, 2026): Neutral, Stern, Pleading, Sad or Glad, each a preset of the printing's rate, the blips' pitch at the line's start and end (falling reads sad, rising pleading), how long each blip rings (short and clipped is stern, long and soft is sad), its tremble, and how many ticks the printing holds after a comma and after a sentence's end (an ellipsis holds once). The moods are mostly tempo and pitch, since a blip voice tires the ear. A node's `mood`, the port's own field on dialogue records which the importer leaves unset, says how its line is said, for a mod to tag; unset, `TextReveal.Guess` reads the text: a shouted word or an exclamation mark is stern, unless the line also asks or pleads; an ellipsis is sad; a question mark or a pleading word (please, beg, help, mercy, spare, must, need) is pleading; a sad word (sorry, alas, dead, died, dying, death, lost, gone, grief, mourn, weep, forgive, never) is sad and a glad one (thank, thanks, wonderful, glad, welcome, splendid, excellent, happy, joy, delighted) glad; else neutral. The same tag is meant to drive faces once remodels carry expression shapes (dev notes).

Not ported yet: the widgets' fade in and out (`fadeFloatMirror`) and scrolling a menu taller than its bounds.

## The pause menu

The System, Party and World tabs (`BigAssMenu.cpp`, `DollMenu*.cpp`), with the Party tab's barter and training states.

- **The tabs are one long scroll.** Each tab is a widget tree in its own 320 × 240 frame, 320 pixels apart, and changing tab slides them all. Over them is a 3D scroll, `aidyn:actor/0x0E6B` (scene `0x0E63`), seen from above, which slides with them: System shows its wooden roller at the left, World at the right, Party neither. It unrolls as the menu opens and rolls up as it closes ([opening and closing](#opening-and-closing)).
- **Each tab has a full-screen image** over the scroll (`PauseMenu.SectionImages`, `0x800de9c0`): `0x00D8`, `0x00D9` and `0x00DA`, all 320 × 240.
- **The menus keep the original's layouts at every screen size** ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-menus-keep-the-originals-layouts.md)): each tab's image is drawn whole at the screen's centre and filled in at the original's coordinates.

### Changing member and list

The menu's widgets animate with tweens (`MenuTween.CreateShort` at `0x800cc880`, `CreateByte` `0x800ccb88`): a value moved from where it is to an end over so many frames, a step a frame, on a curve that eases in and out, 0.5 sin(πt − π/2) + 0.5 (`MenuTween.Ease`, `0x800e6590`), truncated to a whole number each step. A tween takes its frames and finishes on the next, so the widget is busy a frame longer. The port's tweens and fades are `MenuTween` and `MenuMotion.cs`.

- **C-left shows the next member and C-right the one before** (`PartyMenu.CLeft` `0x8003e1c8`, `CRight` `0x8003e1f4`, slots 0xBC and 0xC4 of the menu's handlers, which the C-buttons' bits 0x2 and 0x1 call).
- **The portraits move** (`PartyMenu.PortraitsChange`, `0x8003b3cc`): each member's portrait moves and resizes from its old place to its new one over 15 frames, x, y, width and height each tweened. With C-left, the next member's grows into the large frame, the one shown shrinks into the last small one, and the others move one frame left. The white frames are painted into `0x00D9` and stay. One widget per member, drawn in party order.
- **The rest crossfades over the same 15 frames:** the name; the aspect's and school's icons, where they change; the hit points, their maximum and the level, always (`PartyMenu.HealthGoldChange`, `0x8003be5c`); and the silhouette's slots. The bar's width tweens to the new member's. The gold and the list on the right change at once.
- **The silhouette** (`PartyMenu.EquipmentChange`, `0x8003e374`): the original crossfades each slot whose item differs. The port crossfades a slot only where one member wears something in it and the other doesn't, and changes one worn item to another at once, the user's choice. That choice is the member change's alone: for an item put on or taken off (below) the port crossfades every slot whose item differs, as the original does (`PartyMenu.SlotCrossfades`).
- **Changing the member shown runs the same crossfades.** `HealthGoldChange` doesn't count the numbers up or down: it builds the new hit points, maximum and level as new texts, fades them in from alpha 0 and the old ones out over 15 frames (`MenuTween.CreateByte`, freed at the end by `MenuTween.FadeAndFree`, `0x8003f1b0`), and tweens the bar's width (+0xA0) from where it is to hit points ÷ maximum × 80, only if they differ, over the same 15 (`MenuTween.CreateShort`). Before that it finishes any of its tweens still running, so a second change starts from what's shown. Its other callers are:
  - **A on the inventory** (`PartyMenu.InventoryA`, `0x80035fa0`): whatever isn't refused with a message ends at `0x80036694` with `EquipmentChange` and `HealthGoldChange` for the member shown. So a potion drunk, a scroll read (whatever it says), an item put on or taken off (even one the inventory has no room for, which stays on), and in a battle's pause a flask taken in hand, all crossfade the numbers and the changed slots; a potion that would do nothing, an equip refused and the Archmage Staff's message don't.
  - **A skill's result** (`MenuPopup.ShowResult`, `0x80055ed4`, for the Alchemist, the Mechanic and the Healer's three tasks): the numbers, for the member whose skill it is, who's the member shown, whatever the task did. Laying on hands costs the Healer Stamina, so their hit points drop as the result shows.
  - **A training purchase**, the skills', spells' and stats' (`0x80037ccc`, `0x800391e0`, `0x8003a90c`), at a trainer and after a battle, once the rank has gone up: the numbers, an Endurance point raising the maximum.

  The port keeps the numbers as they stood (`HeaderNumbers`) and the silhouette's worn icons (`PartyMenu.WornIcons`) when the change starts, as `PartyMenu.PreviousNumbers` and `PreviousSlots` with their eased shares `NumbersChange` and `SlotsChange`; `PartyMenu.ChangeNumbers` is the training windows' and the skills' way in. The portraits, name and icons (`Previous`, `MemberChange`) move only for a member change.
- **The stick's left shows the next list and its right the one before** (`PartyMenu.ListsLeft` `0x800319f0`, `ListsRight` `0x80031a10`), wrapping from stats to the inventory. The list, its title and its arrows slide together, the old and the new 140 pixels over 20 frames (`PartyMenu.ChangeList`, `0x80031620`): left brings the next list from x 20 to 160 and pushes the old from 160 to 300; right the other way. The rows are cut to the list's bounds, x 160 to 300 and y 101 to 205, and the title to x 170 to 300 (`PartyMenu.ListsCreate`, `0x80030c50`); the port cuts the title at 160 with the rows.
- **A change waits for the last** (`PartyMenu.Changing`, `0x800304b4`): no member change while the header, the silhouette or the lists are moving, and no list change while the lists are. So for 16 frames after a potion drunk or an item put on, C-left and C-right do nothing. Training's member change animates the same way. **In the port's look nothing waits**, the user's choice (Nov 3, 2026): a member change finishes whatever moves at once and is taken, and a list change the lists' slide (`PartyMenu.Settle`), so no press is lost; and the header changes over in 10 frames, two thirds of the original's 15 (`PartyMenu.PortMemberFrames`). The original's look waits as the original does.

### The port's layout

It's painted far larger than it's drawn, so it's mipmapped and filtered. **The mipmaps are made by coverage** (`CoverageMipmaps`, `UiImages.Mipmapped`), for the carried art, a mod's larger images and the cut portraits: each smaller level covers the whole of the last, an odd side's texels a little more than two each, its colour weighted by alpha, and at every level a clear texel beside the art takes its colour. Godot's own (`Image.GenerateMipmaps`, with `FixAlphaEdges`) left dark smudges on the right and bottom edges of the aspects' and schools' icons, which the user saw (Nov 2, 2026): on art not a power of two square, 250 there, it leaves out an odd side's last row or column, so the GPU, stretching each level over the whole, drew the smaller levels' art further right and down, up to 12% at level 5; and it averaged the greys the icons' clear pixels hold into their edges. Measured on the star's icon at 45 pixels, the ring just outside it on the right was darkened 31 against 7 on the left, and 18 against 10 after.
- **Not done yet:** the shops' and the loot's lists as grids, and figures for the members the man's, big man's and woman's don't fit (the user has drawn a dwarf's and a child's, not yet used), and the user's one-cornered frame, red selection square, hand and quill, descending lines and X, which wait for the screens they're for.

### The model and the layouts

`PartyMenu` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state: the member shown, the list showing with its rows (`PartyMenuRows`) and cursor, the silhouette's slots, and C-down's details (`MenuDetails`). `MenuImages` and `InventoryOrder` hold the chrome's image IDs and the kinds' order, and `Figures` which figure a member is drawn over. `PartyTabView` (`AidynRetold.Views`) draws it with the widget kit, `UiPainter`.

**The Party tab's windows** are `IPartyWindow` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`), each a `WidgetDollMenu` in one of the original's pause states: the tab itself (`PartyMenu`), the loot window and shops (`BarterMenu`), the training window (`TrainingMenu`) and the post-battle window (`PostBattleMenu`, pause state 5, [below](#after-a-battle)). The pause menu sends every press to whichever window is up, and neither it nor the view knows which; the stick's left and right (`Sideways`) are the tab's next or previous list, the loot window's pile or party list, or training's skills or spells. `PartyTabView` draws the header for every window and the window's own parts on a layer over it (`DrawWindow`, with a case for each), whose alpha carries the post-battle windows' fades.

**Lists are laid out in the core.** `ListLayout` is a list's bounds in the 320 × 240 frame and its rows' pitch, which ADR 0014 fixes: the Party tab's 12 rows of 13 ([the port's layout](#the-ports-layout); the original's are 8 of 13 and the stats 9 of 11), barter's pile and training's 6 of 13, crafting's 11 or 10 slim-font lines or 4 members of 28. The menus build their lists with the layout's count, so a list scrolls with no view drawn, and the views draw and hit-test from the same layout rather than repeating the pitch and bounds.

### Opening and closing

`PauseMenu` and `PauseScroll` in the core have the rules, and `PauseMenuView` and `PauseScrollView` in the runtime draw them (the original's `PauseMenu.DarkenStep` at `0x8004847c`, `DrawBackdrop` `0x800484cc`, the menu's `OpenStep` `0x8002f1d0`, `CloseStep` `0x8002f4d4`, `SlideOut` `0x8002e9d0` and `Update` `0x8002ee38`).

- **Everything in the menu steps once a frame.** The menu's frame (`PauseMenu.Frame`) counts its delta, but the widgets, the scroll and the fades take one step a frame whatever it is (`WidgetManager.Update`, `0x800ad10c`). So their times are in frames. The port runs the menu at 60 frames a second (`Cadence.MenuFrameTicks`), which matches the original's timing in the emulator, tweens and pulses alike (`MenuPulse`).
- **The world freezes and darkens first.** Any way into the menu (Start, a shop or trainer's script, a container) copies the frame on screen into a 320 × 240 image (`GetSnapShot`, `0x800208b4`), drawn behind the menu multiplied by a grey (`PauseMenu.BackdropTint`, `0x800d9564`): 255, then 10 less a frame to 128 (the last step 7), 13 frames. The menu is built on the 14th. The copy isn't blurred. Once the menu is open, the original draws black behind it and only the copy's top and bottom 40 lines, which the scroll leaves showing.
- **In the port the world isn't copied but drawn on, still**, the user's choice: the menu stops trek, with the scenery's actors and the free camera, and the world and the HUD go on being drawn as they stand under a black layer at one less the tint. So resizing the window redraws the world at its new shape, and the HUD is laid out again as the Menu size option changes. The HUD stays drawn under the menu and pages: hidden tick by tick, it went missing for a tick as they closed. The port still copies the frame as the menu opens, for a save's screenshot ([below](#saving-and-loading)).
- **The scroll.** The actor's states, by request: idle (0, 11 frames, the rollers at ±35.3, off the frame), opening on System or World (22, 37 frames, the rollers parting from ±2.6), opening on Party (10, 53 frames: the scroll comes up from below rolled, then parts from the middle to about ±13.5, then idles), closing from Party (19, 53) and from System or World (11, 39); 20 and 21 aren't the menu's. The opening is the tab's: barter, training and the loot window open on Party. The original's actors take a request for one step and then go back to 0, so each plays once. The menu sets the scene's x by the tab, 27 for System, 0 for Party and −26 for World (`0x800de9c8`), and slides it with the tabs, 1/32 of the way a frame. Its camera is 27 above the scene, looking straight down with −Z up the screen, 45° over the 240 lines, near 1 and far 256. It's drawn over the tabs. The scene's two lights light it as directional lights, warm from the left and dim blue from the right, with no ambient.
- **The tabs show only where the scroll has unrolled.** Each tab's image and widgets are cut to columns of the frame (`PauseMenu.ClipLeft` and `ClipRight`, `0x800d9568`), in x only, that follow the scroll's frame f in a straight line:

  | Tab | Opening | Closing |

  | System | from the right: the left edge 320 until frame 8, then 0 by 36 | from the left: the left edge 0 at frame 1, 320 by 31 |
  | Party | from the middle: 160 to 160 until frame 36, then 0 to 320 by 55 | to the middle: 0 to 320 at frame 1, 160 to 160 by 14 |
  | World | from the left: the right edge −5 until frame 6, then 320 by 35 | from the right: the right edge 320 at frame 2, 0 by 32 |

  Each opens when its animation ends: Party at frame 53, its clip 17 to 303, its tab showing from frame 37; System and World at 37. Only then does the menu take input. The original's clips stop short of the frame's edges, behind the overscan. The port shows those columns, so a side within 17 columns of the frame's edge counts as at it (`PauseScroll.Overscan`), and it also cuts the tabs at the rollers, along their middles (`PauseScrollView.Arrange`), so the parchment never shows past one; a side at the frame's edge reaches the roller on that side. The user chose both.
- **Parchment fills the space between the rollers,** the user's choice (Oct 31, 2026; `ScrollParchmentView`). The original's clip lags the rollers as they part and leads them as they close, so the world showed between them as the scroll began to open and finished closing. Behind the tabs the port draws each tab's plain parchment (the veil's, or a mod's blank parchment), cut only at the rollers' middles. So there's parchment between them whenever they stand across the screen, widening as they part, and each tab's painted layout and widgets come in over it as the original's clip lets them. Up and down it's cut to the rows the rollers' ends reach (`PauseScrollView.Arrange`). Each end is drawn in by the roller's torn end, the last 4% of its length, and by the height its round end shows as it leans toward the camera (its radius times the lean), so it doesn't stand above them where their ends dip between them. As the Party scroll comes up from below, its rollers seen end on and then standing up by frame 30, it fades in as they stand, from 0.35 to 0.7 upright (1 less the axis's part toward the camera); a flat strip between rollers still leaning toward the camera stood out of them like a card. It fades out as they lean back closing.
- **The parchment runs the screen's height,** the user's choice (Oct 31, 2026): the original's tabs leave the darkened world showing above and below their torn edges, rows 13 to 33 at the top and 198 to 226 at the bottom. Each tab's image is made taller (`TallImage`, `UiImages.Tall`, drawn by `UiPainter.Tab`): a column at a time, the parchment unbroken through its middle keeps its place, so everything painted in stays under the widgets drawn over it, and its torn top edge and rim, with any frayed flap past it, move up 7 lines and the bottom 12: the System tab's edge then reaches lines 6 and 234, where the user marked the parchment's edge, the game showing beyond it, and the other tabs keep their own tears. The gaps are the tab's own plain parchment (`PlainImage`, the veil's), from the same rows 80 inside in every column, so they run on across columns and from tab to tab as the images do, fading over 6 rows into the parchment at either end. The menu's text and pictures are as they were. The remodelled scroll's rolls, in the ACRE plugin, are lengthened past the screen at both ends, a little beyond the parchment ([replacing graphics](../replacing-graphics.md#swapping-a-parts-mesh-and-keeping-its-animations)); the original's rolls stop at the original's torn edges, short of the taller parchment.
- **The game's blank parchment takes the place of the tabs' own,** the user's choice and the user's art (Oct 31, 2026): `acre:image/parchment`, 2560 × 1920, its torn top and bottom transparent, the ACRE plugin's (`ParchmentSwap`, `UiImages.OnParchment`, drawn by `UiPainter.Tab` in place of the tab's image). A plugin's PNG beside the scroll's model, `assets/aidyn/scene/0x0E63.parchment.png` at any size, takes its place. The tabs' own images, made taller (`TallImage`) or plain (`PlainImage`), are drawn only if neither can be read. Nothing painted into the tabs is drawn on it, as the user wanted it blank, so what the original paints rather than draws is gone with the old parchment: the Party tab's HitPoints, Level and Gold labels, the slots' frames and silhouette, the knotwork and "Details", and the World tab's map backdrop, compass and the calendar's days and moons. What the menus draw over the tabs stays. It's laid across each tab's frame eight times as fine as the tab, as it's painted (`ParchmentSwap.Scale`; it was four, half the painting's own detail), its width across the frame's and its outermost torn edges on lines 6 and 234. The Party tab's is mirrored (`ParchmentSwap.Mirrored`), so the paper runs on unbroken from tab to tab, its left edge against its left and its right against its right, where three copies side by side showed a seam at each join. It's laid on another thread as the tabs' views are made, once per plugin set and side, and the first draw waits for it if it hasn't finished; it's mipmapped for a screen drawn at the original's size. The remodelled scroll's rolls, in the ACRE plugin, are painted with the same image ([replacing graphics](../replacing-graphics.md#the-pause-menus-parchment)); the model stays a plugin's, outside the repo, as it carries the original's scene, its textures and animations.
- **On screens wider and taller than the frame**, the port changes what the original's scroll does beyond it, the user's choice (`PauseScroll`, `PauseScrollView`):
  - **The animations run on from one another.** The Party opening's last 9 frames are the System and World opening's first 9, and the System and World closing's frames 31 to 39 are the Party closing's 1 to 9. So the Party tab's scroll runs on into the System and World opening at its frame 10 (`ActorStateMachine.Join`), its rollers going out to the ends of the System and World tabs rather than jumping there once off the frame. The menu still takes input from frame 53. Closing from Party, the System and World closing brings the rollers back in to the frame's edges, then the Party closing runs from its first frame.
  - **Fades.** The Party scroll fades in over its opening's first 12 frames, and out over its closing's last 12, as it comes up from below the frame. The System and World tabs and their scroll fade out together from their closing's frame 29, gone by its end.
  - **Each roller has its own camera** past the frame, following it, its picture moving out with the tabs a tab's 320 pixels a tab. One camera over the scene's middle won't do: the scene's x steps aren't quite 320 pixels at the rollers' depth, so a roller further out drifts and widens over its tab.
  - **Barter and training** can't change tab, so their scroll runs on only to frame 11 of the System and World opening and holds there, the rollers just past what the Party tab draws.
  - **The scroll's viewports don't interpolate** between physics ticks, or its first frame would show where it was reset to.
- **Closing,** the scroll rolls up at once (`SlideOut` steps it), and when its animation ends the world brightens 10 a frame back to 255, 13 frames, before trek goes on. The loot window and shops (Game.Mode 7) snap back to full brightness instead. Camping waits for the menu to close; loading a save closes it at once.
- **How it ends** is its exit code (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`, a jump table at `0x800d1240`), which the trek pause menu leaves as it rolls up and which sets trek's next state as the slide ends (`PauseMenu.NextTrekState`, `0x800df031`, copied at `0x8004898c`): 0 for B or Start (trek runs on, state 3), 1 for Camp (state 0x11, the camp), 2 for the Theatre's cinematic (states 0x16 and 0x17), 3 for Help (state 0x12, script `0x054B`), 4 once a save has loaded (state 4, trek built again from it) and 5 for the credits (state 0x19). The loot window, shops and training close straight to trek (`PauseMenu.CloseToTrek`, `0x80048f40`). In the port the pages return the exit (`PauseExit`, a save's path among them), the core's `PauseMenu` returns it from its `Step` once the menu has closed, and the session carries it out ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The post-battle windows** (game mode 6) open with the Party scroll's opening over the black the battle left, and the loot window that follows closes with its closing roll; between them the post-battle window's lists fade out and the loot window's in, 60 a frame (`PauseMenu.CloseFade`, `OpenFade`), the scroll staying open ([below](#after-a-battle)).
- **Each opening starts the tab afresh** on the inventory's top row, but on the member last shown, so the presses to reach something are the same every time from the same member.
- **The member** every Party window opens on, the Party tab, barter, training and the post-battle windows alike, is one byte, `PauseMenu.Member` (`0x800DE9F0`), which `DollMenu::InitMenu` (`0x800300B0`) builds them on. Only three places in 1.0 store to it: C-left and C-right in any of them (`PartyMenu.ChangeMember`, `0x8003054C`, stores at `0x800305C8` and `0x800305F0`), a battle's pause from a turn, which sets the member whose turn it is (`Combat.StatePause`, store at `0x800226D0`; the pause from any other state, `0x80022930`, leaves it), and `InitMenu` itself, which steps back from an empty slot to the one before with a member and keeps that (`0x80030128`). So it outlasts trek, combat, the windows and a save loaded, and it isn't saved: it's 0 at power-on. The port keeps it in the core's `PauseMenu`, which the session holds. **Where the port differs:** loading a save builds a new session, and with it a new menu, so the member goes back to Alaron and the remembered tab ([changing tab](#changing-tab)) to System; whether loading resets the original's tab isn't settled. Carrying them across a load is open ([roadmap](../roadmap.md)).
- **Input** is translated once into a `MenuAction` (`MenuStick.Translate`), by the controls of the situation the game is in ([controls](#controls)), and what a press does is the core's: `GameSession.Press` gives it to the mode that runs ([runtime](runtime.md)). Trek's (`TrekLoop.Press`) goes to a backdrop's page, a map object's popup or a conversation first, and otherwise Start opens the menu, A uses the map object reached and B raises the portraits, none of them while trek fades into a cut-scene or goes through a teleporter. The editor's panels press the same `IPartyWindow.Press`, so the two can't drift.
- **The stick counts once as it crosses a threshold** (`MenuStick`). The original's menus count a direction from 56 of the N64 stick's 80, 0.7 (`Controller::ReadInput`, `0x800915d8`); ares scales a full push to about 100, so the port counts from 0.58 and lets go below 0.5. **Held,** the stick or the D-pad repeats after 30 ticks and then every 5: the original's widgets count 30 of the controller's 60 samples a second, then 5 (`BaseWidget::Control`, `0x800ad708`), for the stick, the D-pad, C-up and C-down. The session's `StickRepeat` counts the ticks, so a hold repeats on the same ticks whatever the frame rate: in the pause menu a repeat moves the cursor (not while it opens, closes or slides); in trek's popups, conversations and backdrops, a cut-scene and combat's held menus it's a press; and where nothing has a cursor the hold is forgotten, so the next doesn't start by repeating. A keyboard key repeats as the system repeats it.

### The Party tab's actions

What A does (`DollMenuLists::AFunc`), in `PartyMenu.Actions`, with the popups as `MenuMessage` and `CraftingMenu` and `PartyTabView` drawing them.

- **The inventory** (`WidgetInvShop::AFunc`). A on a worn item takes it off into the inventory, staying on if the inventory has no room, which the original does silently and the port says, "No room in inventory.", the user's choice (Nov 2, 2026). The Archmage Staff stays, and says it "contains powerful magic that only" its wielder "can control" (`XPowerfulMagic`). A on a carried item: a potion is drunk by the member shown ([items](items.md#potions)); a scroll is read, which says how it went whatever happens (`Party::UseScroll`); anything else is equipped on the member shown, putting back what it replaces ([items](items.md#equipment)). A refusal says why, from the equip error strings (`Party.EquipErrors`, common strings 95–101): the aspect, Strength or Intelligence it needs, the original's "the minimum PS required" and "IN" spelled out, "Strength" and "Intelligence", in the port's look, the user's choice (Nov 2, 2026; `PartyMenu.SpelledOut`), or else "You cannot equip that item!", which is also what too many rings, no free gear slot and no room for what it replaces say (`GetEquipError3` and the item kinds' functions return 1). The port's own refusal, replacing the staff, uses the staff's message. After equipping, the cursor stays on what's left of the stack, or on its row, which the next item moves up into, so it never jumps up to the worn items; after taking off, it stays on its row. Equipping the last item of the list, when no item moves up into its row, leaves the cursor on the item that was second-last, as the user saw in 1.0; the port clamps it to the list's last row, which is that item.
- **The skills** (`WidgetSkillInfo::AFunc`, trek only): A on the Alchemist, Mechanic or Healer opens their popup; on any other skill, a spell or a stat it does nothing. The Mechanic with no hide for a recipe their base level reaches says "You do not have the components to create any armor." instead (`makeArmorMenu`).
- **A popup takes every press** while it's open, as the original's widget handler gives them to the top widget: the stick moves its cursor, A chooses, B closes it, and the member and list don't change. `MenuOverlay` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is that rule once, for the popups every Party window, the World tab and the save screens keep: a message, the help, a question over choices (`MenuChoice`) and C-down's details. The newest, in the order message, help, choice, details, takes the press, and closing it hands the next to the one under it. The Party tab's crafting popup and the drop question (`BarterConfirm`) are the menus' own, beside it.
- **The popups' fades** (`PopupFade`). The skills' popups (the Mechanic's `0x80002a08` from `PartyMenu.MakeArmorMenu`, `0x80002890`; the Alchemist's `0x80002fc4`; the Healer's `MakeHealerMenu`, `0x800034a0`; all through `MenuPopup.Menu`, `0x80055c98`) and C-down's details (`PartyMenu.InventoryDetails` `0x80036710`, `SpellDetails` `0x80037994`, `SkillDetails` `0x80038ea4` and `StatDetails` `0x8003a7d0`, through `MenuPopup.Create`, `0x800337bc`) are one widget family, `itemdetail.cpp`'s, and fade alike:
  - **In.** Each opens at alpha 0 (`MenuPopup.Open`, `0x800336a0`), and each frame steps it before it's drawn: 60 more while it's under 195, else 255, where it's open (`MenuPopup.FadeIn`, `0x80032840`). So it's drawn at 60, 120, 180, 240 and 255.
  - **Out.** 60 less while it's 61 or more, else it's freed before it's drawn (`MenuPopup.FadeOut`, `0x800328ac`): 195, 135, 75 and 15. B on a popup starts it (`MenuPopup.OnB`, `0x80055e10`), and so does closing the message with a popup's result. The details start it once they're open and C-down isn't held (`PartyMenu.CloseDetails`, `0x80034c18`), tested only then, so a quick tap still fades them fully in and straight out.
  - **One alpha for the whole popup.** `Widget.SetAlpha` (`0x800b2928`) sets it on the parchment, its shadow, the title, the rows, the icons and the cursor's row, whose pulse it multiplies (`0x800afe88`). The port draws the popup and the details each on a layer whose modulate is the alpha.

While the details show, they take the presses and do nothing with them, and C-down over a popup still fading in shows nothing.
  - **The loot window, a shop and training** have the same details, with the same fades.
  - **The Healer's lists change in the same popup**, with no fade: a task's row clears the popup's title and list and builds the members' in their place (`Healer.ShowLayOnHandsList` `0x800038e4`, `ShowHerbList` `0x80003ab8`, `ShowRestoreMembers` `0x80003c84`, each through `Healer.ClearList` `0x80004304`), and a member's row under Restore the attributes' (`Healer.ShowRestoreStats`, `0x800040fc`). - **A crafting popup** (`WidgetCrafting`, `WidgetMenuPopup`) is on the details' parchment at (0, 58), in the slim font: its title in the rows' colour centred on x 93, 70 down, and its list from x 23 to 163 under it, 122 tall for the Alchemist and 117 for the rest. ### The loot window

  - **Images** are the original's, the first plugin's, whatever a mod puts over them (`UiImages.Get`, `PluginSet.FindOriginalAsset`), so ReChronicled's redrawn art gives way to the original's pixels.
  - **The Party tab** is the original's layout (`PartyMenu.OriginalLook`, `PartyTabView`'s `OriginalLook` branches): `0x00D9` with its frames painted in, the portraits in them, the aspect's and school's icons under the large one, 2 apart; the hit points bar (`0x00E0`) and the hit points, level and gold on the labels at (160, 22) (`WidgetHealthGold`); the silhouette's slots in their painted frames from (18, 72); the list under its title, eight rows of 13 from (160, 101) to (300, 205) (`PartyMenu.OriginalList`), the inventory a list with what's worn first, sliding 140 (`OriginalListDistance`), cut to (160, 78)–(300, 206); the arrow under the knotwork at (126, 207); C-down's details on the original's parchment, with no flourish; and the barter, training and post-battle windows where the original has them, with no tabs. The mouse works as it does in the port's layout.
  - **The System list** is the original's, Theater, Credits and Help in it, with the port's Quit Game last (`SystemMenu.TrekItems`); it follows a change of Graphic Quality as the options are left.
  - **What stays the port's:** the options' pages, which hold the settings the original hasn't, Graphic Quality among them; the screen's settings; and the World tab's, the HUD's and the conversation box's layouts, which take the original's text, boxes and images but aren't laid out again, so what the port changed in them stays.
- **Not settings any more**, the user's choice (Nov 2, 2026): Menu Filter (always Sharp), Pace (the original's; `--pace` and F5 still quicken a run, [movement](movement.md)) and Jump (always on). Older settings' `uiFilter`, `briskPace` and `jump` are passed over as they're read.
- **The Video page lists what the screen allows.** The runtime says which screen the window is on as the options open (`IPauseMenuHost.Screen`, a `ScreenInfo`), and the page's rows are made again after each change, so Window Size and Render Resolution follow the display mode and the window's size. Godot 4 can't change the screen's own mode, so there's no exclusive fullscreen and no choice of the screen's resolution or refresh rate; Window Size and Frame Rate Limit stand in for them, the user's choice.
- **Window Size is the window as it is**, the user's choice. Choosing a size always changes the window, restoring it first if it's maximized, and resizing, maximizing or restoring the window by hand changes the setting: `Game.WindowChanged` takes the window's size (`Settings.WindowWidth`, `WindowHeight`) or that it's maximized (`Settings.Maximized`, which keeps the size it's restored to), kept half a second after the last change of a drag, and tells an open Video page (`OptionsMenu.Refresh`), which lists a size dragged to among the others. Fullscreen and minimizing leave it be.
- **The sky's placement** is off the menu, the user's choice: the projected sky is always drawn, and F6 or `--sky original` draws the original's for comparing ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)).
- **How a setting takes effect** is the runtime's ([runtime](runtime.md), [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-the-core-is-handed-its-settings-each-tick.md)): the menu changes the player's `Settings`, which nothing else in the core reads. The command line's settings for a run lie over the player's and are never saved ([runtime](runtime.md#the-command-line)), so the page shows the player's own.
- **The original's resolution row** switches to the Expansion Pak's high resolution (512 × 240) and back; without the pak, it's greyed and the cursor skips it. "32 Bit Color" is in the table, but nothing reaches it.
- **The original keeps its volumes and resolution in every save** (`QuestData::Save`), so loading set them back. The port keeps the settings per player instead.

  **The mouse in the menus and conversations** is the port's, on a PC. Hovering a row of the System and Party tabs, the title's menu and the quit box puts the cursor on it, the wheel moves it, and a click on a row chooses it, as A does (`UiScreen.Clicked`, which the runtime presses as A); a click that misses the rows chooses nothing, so a stray click can't camp or quit. The World tab, the shops and the other popups take no clicks yet. In a conversation, a cut-scene's box, a popup and a backdrop's page, the mouse over the box's choices points at them (`DialogueBox.PointAt`) and a click anywhere moves on, as A does; a right click is Back everywhere, as a binding.

  **Cut-scenes and conversations take the menus' controls** ([ADR 0027](../adr/0027-conversations-take-the-menus-controls.md)), as do trek's popups, a backdrop's and the help menu's page, a cinematic and the credits: Move Cursor moves the choices, Accept continues, Back closes a popup, goes back on the help menu's page or skips a cinematic, and Close Menu skips a cut-scene, a cinematic or the credits. They're live in both situations (`Situation.Talking` is still what those modes are), so a rebound Accept is a conversation's too and the page can't say one thing while a conversation does another; the menus' other controls don't reach them. A battle's turn walks and turns the camera with trek's controls; Sneak still acts there, as it always has, but isn't checked against combat's buttons, since the left trigger is also Skip Turn. The debug keys (the F-keys, F and the free camera's Space and C, fast-forward on either stick's click) aren't on the page and stay fixed.
- **The layout:** the Device row (Keyboard, Controller) at y 70, then the list from y 88, nine lines 12 apart, a situation's heading taking a line of its own, centred in a darker brown. Labels are at x 72 and values at x 188, clear of the longest label, Previous Member, to 304; a value longer than that is printed in the slim font, as a save's long name is. The list scrolls to keep the cursor's row in the middle, with the System list's arrow `0x3757` turned up or down at the right where there's more. Under the list, at y 202, the last change's message for three seconds, or on a binding what clears it ("R: Clear"). A table with a column for each device was tried first, but it doesn't fit the 320-pixel frame: the longest label (104 pixels in the main font) and two cells as wide as "Left Stick, D-Pad" (101) need over 300 of the 238 between the roller and the frame's edge. Mouse Sensitivity (0.25x to 4x; at 1x mouse look turns about 0.12° a pixel, and the drag 0.3°) is on the keyboard's page, then Reset to Defaults, which resets both devices.
- **A button's row:** A shows "Press a Key... 5" (or "Press a Button...") in its value, the row steady in the headings' brown, counting the seconds down. The next key, mouse button or wheel turn (on the keyboard's page), or controller button, stick or trigger pushed past 0.6 (on the controller's), replaces the cell, and the prompt closes. Nothing else ends it but the five seconds, which change nothing, so Escape, Backspace, Start and B can be bound like any other; an input of the other device and the debug keys' F-keys are passed over. While it waits it takes every event, before the menus (`Game.BindPrompted`), and the System tab's mouse handling lets the wheel and buttons by. The menus' Appraise (R, X) clears the cell.
- **A direction's row** (Move, Camera, Move Cursor, Target) is a choice among its sources' combinations on the device: the stick's left and right or A step through None, each source, and each combination, "Both Sticks" for the two sticks. The mouse is only Camera's; Move Cursor never offers None.
- **Conflicts** are checked only within a situation, and the newest binding wins: whatever else in the same situation had the input loses it, a button its binding and a direction the source holding it, and the foot says "Taken From Portraits". Two directions may share a source, which is how Camera Hold works. A battle's aiming (`Situation.Aiming`) is apart from its turn, so RB is both Camera Hold on a turn and Ranged Right when aiming.
- **No lockout:** the essential controls, trek's Menu, the menus' Move Cursor, Accept and Back, and combat's Pause, always keep a binding on each device. Clearing one is refused ("Accept Needs a Binding"), and so is a rebind that would take one's last binding ("Accept Needs It"); a `settings.json` that leaves one with none gets its default back. Without these the player couldn't open the menu, move on its pages, or leave a conversation.
- **The file** keeps only the controls that differ from their defaults, each with all its bindings, under `controls`; unknown controls, bindings that aren't one of the forms and the debug keys are skipped as it loads. Each cell is replaced whole, so the page never adds a second key; the defaults' Enter and Escape come back with Reset.

#### Renaming Alaron

Rename Character opens the name entry (`InputMenu`) over the options. It renames Alaron, and the save keeps his name (`SaveParty::SaveAlaron`, see [saves](saves.md)). Scripts' `{player}` and the cinematics' captions use it ([scripts](scripts.md#its-boundary)).

- **The layout:** the title `0x00F9` centred at y 50, and the name centred at y 85 in (130, 80, 80). Below them the grid at (60, 110), 13 keys a row, 15 apart: A–Z, 0–9, then the symbol keys, which the font draws as its own symbols (a skull, a heart, a diamond for the underscore), then rub out, cancel and OK. The cursor is `0x00F8`, a white square 15 px across, pulsing grey 5 a step between 95 and 230 from 150, 54 frames a cycle (`InputMenu.Step`, `0x8002d7e4`), with its key in black on it. The letter being chosen pulses the same way (`InputMenu.Draw`, `0x8002d464`); where the square's own tint comes from isn't traced, and the port gives it the same. The port marks the next letter's place with a short line.
- **The keys:** the stick moves over the grid, wrapping round its rows, and writes the key into the slot being chosen. A types it and moves on, but not past the tenth (`IMNameLength`). Start goes to OK, and a second Start confirms. B cancels. The first letter and any letter after a space are capitals, the rest small. The cursor starts on OK, so Start, Start keeps the name.
- **The letter in the slot is only shown until A types it**, the user's choice: moving onto rub out, cancel or OK puts back what the slot held, so an empty slot shows its line again and a full name's tenth letter comes back, and Start's jump to OK, rubbing out and confirming all leave it out. Before, the last character passed over stayed in the slot, and OK kept it.
- **On a PC** the keyboard types into the name: a printable key types its character, Backspace rubs out the last letter, Enter confirms and Escape cancels, before any of them reach the menu's actions.
- **Not ported:** the cheat codes, which the original checks the name against here (`Cheats::Check`; see [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

#### Saving and loading

One saved game at a time, which the stick's left and right step through. The positions are absolute in the frame:

- **The title** at (132, 24): Load Game `0x00F1` or Save Game `0x00FF`. On the title screen's box it's centred over the box instead, at y 24.
- **The screenshot**, stretched to 104 × 78 at (73, 52).
- **The party's portraits**, 40 square at x 73, 128, 183 and 238, y 133.
- **The arrow** at (183, 101): right only on the first save, both ways between, left only on the last, and none with one save.
- **The name** at (183, 111) and "Hours: %u:%u:%u" (common 481) at (183, 122). The name is Alaron's, or a save's label, such as the checkpoint it was made from, in the slim font if it's too long for the main one. The hours are the time played: ticks ÷ 216000, then the minutes and seconds. The original prints them unpadded, as in 18:4:28; the port pads them, 18:04:28, the user's choice.
- **The actions** at (237, 180), 11 apart: Load or Save (423, 424), Delete (425) and Exit (426), with the list's colours but over 15 steps, turning at 14, 28 frames a cycle (`SaveMenu.Actions`, `0x8007e658`).

The rules:

- **The slots.** The original's saves are notes on the Controller Pak, up to 16, in the pak's order. The port's are the files in the saves folder, newest first.
- **"Create New Save"** (553), in dark brown where the name goes, offers only Save and Exit. The original puts it last; the port first. It writes at once, to `Save n.json`. With nothing to load, the load screen says "There are no saved games." on one line centred under the title, where the original talks about the Controller Pak, and offers only Exit.
- **Saving over a game asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to save over that game?" (410), with Overwrite and Cancel. Saving says "Game successfully saved." (422) with Continue (176), which leads back to the System tab's list, showing the slot just written. The original's "Saving. Do not turn the power OFF…" step isn't needed.
- **Deleting asks first:** "Are you sure you would like to delete that game?" (372), with Delete and Cancel. Deleting takes the screenshot with the save, and starts the list again from the first save.
- **Loading** happens at once, with no question. The menu closes and the game starts again from the save. The runtime reloads its Godot scene with the save, keeping the plugins loaded. A save of a new game, with no place (`acre save new`, the editor's new game), starts as the title's New Game does, with the opening cinematic ([saves](saves.md#the-format)).
- **The questions** are `WidgetChoiceDia` panels 150 wide in off-white, with no B.
- **The screenshot** is taken as the menu opens (`QuestData::TakeSnapshot`). The original crops the frame's centre half, (80, 60) to (240, 180), and samples it down to 64 × 32 at 16 bits, which is why its screenshots look stretched. The port takes the centre half of the window's view, cut to 4:3, at 208 × 156, and saves it beside the save as a PNG of the same name.
- **The slide.** The pages are a widget group (`./menus/widgetgroup.cpp`, `WidgetGroup.Create` at `0x8002b86c`), a box 219 × 192 from the title's corner, (67, 24) to (286, 216), that cuts its pages to it. Each save's page holds its arrow, screenshot, portraits, name and hours (`SaveMenu.AddSlot`, `0x8007e504`); the title and the actions are outside it and stay put, and the actions change at once. Right (`SaveMenu.Right`, `0x8007d498`, then `WidgetGroup.Next`, `0x8002bc68`) slides the page shown out to the left by the box's width as the next comes in from the right, and left the other way (`SaveMenu.Left`, `WidgetGroup.Previous`, `0x8002bd94`). Both are tweens of the page's x over 60 frames on the ease-out curve, sin(π t / 2) (`MenuTween.EaseOut`), truncated to whole pixels. While a page slides, every press is dropped (`SaveMenu.Input`, `0x8007d508`), and the page that slid out goes once the tweens end (`WidgetGroup.Step`, `0x8002ba00`). The stick's left and right play 0x073D whether or not there's a save that way.
- **The port slides in 30 frames** (`SaveMenu.SlideFrames`), half the original's, the user's choice. The presses dropped while they slide make no sound. The sliding pages are drawn with the rest of the tab in the same frame, cut to the box (`SystemTabView.SlidingPages`); drawing them in a control of their own, shown a tick later, flickered.

### The World tab

The pause menu's last tab (`WidgetCalendar`, built by `PauseWidget::BuildCalendarMenu` at `0x8002eca8`, set up at `0x8004069c`, `WorldMenu.WorldMenu`), with the world map (`WidgetMenuWorldMap`, `ShowMap` at `0x80049080`) and the journal (`Journal`, `InitMenu` at `0x80052228`). `WorldMenu` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is the tab's state and `JournalMenu` the journal's, drawn by `WorldTabView`. The map's pieces, the towns and the ring are the world map record (`aidyn:worldmap/0`, [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Painted into `0x00DA`**, with no widget: the sea and coast with no land, the compass rose with its N, the calendar's 28 days in their grid with the moons' discs, and the red arrow at the bottom left, pointing only left. The moons are art: the same discs stand in every half season, and no code computes a phase here.
- **The season's name** at (160, 40), in the main font in (130, 80, 80), wrapping between x 160 and 260, so "THE EARTH" over "SEASON". It's one of twelve, common lines 147–158: two a season, by whether 28 days of the season have gone (`0x8004078c`).
- **Today** is `0x00DC`, a white 16-pixel square drawn 17 square and untinted, at x 160 + 14 × column − 1 or − 2 and y 65 + 14 × row. The day of the half season, d from 1 to 28, is at column (d + 2) mod 7 and row (d + 2) div 7. The 1 or 2 is from a table at `0x800d0b50` by row × 7 + column: 1 for 4, 8, 9, 11–18, 21, 23, 26, 28 and 29, 2 for the rest.
- **The world map** (at (0, 0)) is its pieces: `WorldMapPieces` (`0x800df040`), eighteen of flag, image, x and y, each drawn with its top-left at (x, y), unscaled, in the table's order, in (130, 80, 80), while its flag is set. **The fog is the pieces' absence.** The pieces fade in, their alpha rising 5 a tick to 250 and then 255, each time the tab is slid to (the slide resets it), and only while it isn't sliding. Some key items set one ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)), and so does entering a chunk of the region: below.
- **The regions are found by entering them** (`ZoneEngine.EnterRegion`, `0x8004960c`, at the end of `LoadGameBorgScenes`, `0x8000b5f4`): as trek's world is built (`InitZoneEngine`), on every crossing into another chunk and every teleport (`ConfirmPlayerWithinZone` queues one, `TeleportPlayer`), after the pause menu (`PauseMenu.Leave`) and as a cut-scene's stage loads (`CutScene.LoadStage`). The prologue's forest and Gwernia, H08 to N08, are all region 1 (788). The table names no map set, so another set's chunk at a listed row and column counts too; that and its two rows for chunks the Overworld lacks are in [hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md). Nothing clears a region's flag.

#### The journal

- **The book** is `0x0182`, 320 × 240 and opaque, over the tab, which keeps drawing beneath. It fades in 60 a frame (60, 120, 180, 240, 255) and out the same way. **A, B or Start** close it, back to the tab.
- **The entries** are a list from (64, 30), bounded to x 245 and y 220, in the main font wrapping at 181, 11 a line, 4 between entries. A blank line comes first and last. An entry's text is in (98, 70, 60); the newest's in (130, 80, 140). With none, "No entries have been made in the journal." (common 496) in the same brown (`0x80052310`).
- **Which, and in what order.** The original lists every entry whose flag is set, in its table's order, reading each entry's own flag by the number its text begins with (`Journal.AddEntry`, `0x8005256c`). The port lists them in the order they were added ([flags](flags.md), [saves](saves.md#the-format)), the user's choice, and then any whose flag is set but which the network hasn't added yet, as after a battle or a conversation, in the table's order (`WorldMenu`). The flags are plain but entry 32's, the lighthouse's, which is derived 5017, so reading it brings the network up to date as the journal opens, adding those entries and raising the notice, which comes once the menu has closed ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).
- **The newest** is the entry whose flag last turned on (`newestJournal`, set by `FlagNetwork.SetNewestJournal`, `0x80020ea4`). Opening the journal shows it in purple and forgets it (`Journal::Load`, `0x8005263c`, which takes the newest before it lists, and forgets it as that entry is listed). An entry the opening itself adds isn't purple then, but is the newest the next time. Loading turns every entry on again ([saves](saves.md#loading-as-the-original-does)), yet the original shows none purple after a load; how it forgets isn't known. The port leaves none the newest once a save has loaded (`FlagNetwork.Restore`).
- **It opens at the bottom** when the list is taller than 190, and otherwise at the top. In chronological order, the newest is at the bottom anyway.
- **Scrolling is by entry**: down brings the next entry whose foot is below 220 up until its foot meets 220, and up the first whose top is above 30 down until it meets 30, never past either end. The list slides 5 pixels a frame, and a press waits for it. Held, a direction keeps scrolling with no wait (`Journal::Control` falls back to the held bits).
- **The edges fade** into the page: strips of the book redrawn over the text from x 62 to 245, at 0.75, 0.5, 0.25 and 0.125 of its alpha from y 28 to 40, and the same from 220 up to 210.
- **The arrows**, `0x00D6` (8 × 36) at (247, 30) and `0x00D5` at (247, 184), in (130, 80, 80), each fading 30 a frame in while there's more that way: above 30 for the first item, below 220 for the last item (by the count). They draw only at the book's full alpha.
- **A new entry's notice**: when flag 3617 is set in trek, with no fade or popup up, the game clears it and says "A new journal entry has been added.", a literal in the code, in trek's message box (`NewJournalEntryPopup`, `0x80020eb0`; `TrekLoop` in the port). 3617 is a plain flag that the network sets as it comes up to date, which reading it doesn't do ([map objects](map-objects.md#map-objects-write-flags)), so a trigger's entry is told the tick after, and one a script or a battle sets when something next reads a derived flag. The port brings the network up to date before it looks, so every entry is told on trek's first tick with nothing up, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)). The original never says it after loading, though loading sets 3617; the port clears it once a save has loaded ([saves](saves.md)).

#### In the port

### The credits

The credits widget (game mode 0x13, `Credits.Frame` at `0x8001f648`, `Credits.Start` at `0x8002c0b0`, `Credits.Step` at `0x8002c6d8`), which the System tab's Credits row plays, and the ending after the last cinematic ([cinematics](cinematics.md#what-starts-them)). `CreditsRoll` in the core, which the session steps and ends (`GameSession.Credits`), drawn by `CreditsLayer`, from the credits record (`aidyn:credits/0`), which carries the cards, the number shown one at a time and "The End".

- **The way in:** the row (id 9999 in `pauseMenuSelectCallBack`, `0x800403ac`) sets `Credits.Source` to 1 and the menu exits with code 5 (`PauseMenu.Exit`, `0x80048e64`): it rolls up as closing does, then trek's state 0x19 returns mode 0x13. There's no fade: the screen cuts to black, filled every frame (`Screen.FillRect`), and the trek is held as it stood, not freed.
- **The cards.** `Credits.Start` cuts the credits' string table (table 10) into 33 cards by a table of their first lines in the code (`Credits.CardStarts`, `0x800CF060`, ROM `0xCFC60`); a card's first line is its heading, in yellow (225, 225, 0), and the rest its names, in white (225, 225, 225). There's no marker in the text: THQ Credits and Studio X are cards of a heading alone. Each line is a text widget in the main font (`WidgetClipText`), 11 pixels a line, centred across at (320 − width) / 2; three names start with a newline, which leaves a blank line above them. The table's last line, "The End", is the ending's alone.
- **The first 12 cards** (H2O Credits to Lead Tester) show one at a time (`Credits.StepCards`, `0x8002c7b8`): each is moved up from the screen's foot by half its height and the screen's, which centres it, fades in by 4 × delta a frame, holds for 120 / delta frames and fades out by 4 × delta, and the next is set up on the frame after. They stop when the card ending at line 29 (a literal `0x1D`), Designers, comes up: it's the first to scroll.
- **The rest scroll** (`Credits.StepScroll`, `0x8002ca64`), every one starting with its top at the screen's foot. Every other frame, the first card still on screen moves up a pixel, and each after it too once the one before has its foot 32 pixels above the screen's, so the cards come 32 apart. A card that's gone off the top is passed. This counts frames, not ticks; the original runs it at 60 frames a second, and so does the port. The scroll ends when Special Thanks has gone off the top, on a black screen.
- **The end:** from the System tab, the credits end there (`Credits.StepEnd`, `0x8002cb50`). From the ending, "The End" shows in white at y 114, centred, fading in by 2 × delta a frame, holding 240 frames and fading out, and then the game goes back to the title.
- **A, B and Start** end them at once, with no fade (`Credits.Skip`, `0x8002cd90`); the ending's can't be skipped.
- **The music:** `Credits.Start` plays 0x05D9 (the sounds record's `Credits`), looped, at the music volume as it is then, and sets the sound and music volumes to 0, so trek's music and the map's sounds go on silently under it (`ProcessAudioBubbles` keeps running; `GameAudio.Muted` in the port). As they end (`Credits.Destroy`, `0x8002c5a0`) 0x05D9 stops at once and the volumes are set back. The calendar and the weather stand still; the play time counts on.
- **The way out:** the System tab's credits return mode 9: trek fades in from black over 30 ticks (`0x800ce214`, 1/30), with input ignored, and then the menu opens on the System tab's list with the cursor on Load Game, rolling in as it always does. The original skips the fade-in the first time after the console starts (its flag `0x800db06c` is 0 only then); the Theatre's way back shares this. The port always fades in.
- **In the port** they're drawn on a UI screen of their own, as tall as the menu's lines, with the cards centred across and down it.
- **After the ending's**, the game goes back to the title, its intro cards first ([the title screen](#coming-back-to-it)).

## The title screen

The intro cards and the title (`Title.Frame` `0x8001b154`, then game mode 0xF, `Title.ModeFrame` `0x800551c0`), with its menu (`IntroMenu`, built by `IntroMenu.Build` `0x800429b8`). `TitleScreen` and `IntroCards` in the core, which the session shows over a new game's session (`GameSession.ShowTitle`) and steps as its own mode, `GameMode.Title`; `TitleLayer` and `Game.Title.cs` in the runtime draw it. The user's captures are `roms/intro-*.png` and `roms/menu-main2*.png`.

### Power-on

`App.Init` (`0x8001ac7c`) sets `App.State` (`0x800d808c`) to 5, and `HandleAppFrame` switches on it: 5 checks the region (a PAL console gets Borg8 `0x37C3`, "This game is not designed for use on this system", for ever) and the controller (none: "WARNING NO CONTROLLER", `0xFB`, state 4, for ever); 3 is the Controller Pak's check (`PakCheck.Frame`, `0x80082280`: Start held at boot opens its manager, and a pak with no note and fewer than 28 free pages gets the Controller strings' screens); then 0, the cards, and 1, the game's modes, whose first call is `Game.Restart` (`0x8001f284`): mode 0xF, the title, fading in. The port has none of the checks: its saves are files. While the engine starts, before the cards, both apps show the port's full logo on black as Godot's boot splash (`application/boot_splash`, `res://splash.png`, fitted to the window), the user's choice (ADR 0033).

## The trek HUD

Trek's HUD (`TrekHud.Draw`, `0x80020bd4`) is the sundial at the bottom left, the party's portraits at the bottom centre, and at the bottom right the minimap where the player's chunk has a piece, or else the compass under an outdoor sky (sky type 3), or nothing. `TrekHud` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is the state, with `Sundial`, `Compass`, `PartyPortraits` and `Minimap`; `TrekLoop` owns it and `HudView` draws it.

- **Drawn only in trek frames**, and not while a conversation or sign shows (`diaClass->menu`), the pause menu, a shop, training or a cut-scene. Popups draw over it. Every colour and alpha is the scene's brightness times its own, so it fades with the screen.
- **The images** are texture rectangles at quarter-pixel positions, texel × colour, blended (`borg8DlistInit` with flag 6). The rings and the frames have their feathering painted in.

### The sundial

`Sundial::Draw` (`0x800248a4`), centred on (49, 199):

- **The ring** `0x001B` (60 square) at (19, 169), white at alpha 80.
- **The moon** at (39, 189), in (160, 160, 220) at alpha 160, by its phase ([rendering](rendering.md#the-skys-objects)): new `0x01B8`, crescent `0x01B3` or `0x01B4` and quarter `0x01B6` or `0x01B7` in the first and second halves of the moon's four weeks (`(week & 3) >> 1`), full `0x01B5`. It shows at every hour.
- **The rays** `0x01BA` (50 square) at (24, 174), at alpha 80, in the hour's colour.
- **The sun** goes round the ring **twice a day**, 30° an hour clockwise from the top, so noon and midnight are both at the top: its centre is (49 + 25 sin a, 199 − 25 cos a), a in whole degrees, (int)(time of day × 720) mod 720. By day it's the glow `0x01BB`, by night the small dot `0x01B9`, each 20 square.
- **The hour's colour**, for the rays and the sun: from 7 to 21 peach (255, 190, 150), the glow at alpha 160 and no dot; from 23 to 5 blue (120, 100, 190), the dot at 160 and no glow. At dawn (5 to 7) and dusk (21 to 23) the colour blends linearly between them, and the glow and dot cross-fade at 80 an hour. The original dims day's and night's colour by the scene's brightness but not the blend's.
- **The moon and sun hide in caves** and interiors: under sky type 4 (terrains 6, 8, 9, 11 and 13–16, the port's terrains that don't draw the sky). The original decides only on crossing from a chunk under one sky to the other (`TeleportPlayer` at `0x8000bfd0`, through `Sundial::ToggleSun` and `ToggleMoon`, whose names are swapped), and trek starting shows them wherever it is (`Sundial::Init`), so after loading in a cave they show until the player leaves and comes back. **The port fixes it**, deciding by the sky when trek starts too, the user's choice. The Detect Moon Phase and Detect Sun Phase spells write to show them too (`0x800703c8`), but they're cast only in a battle, where trek's sundial is gone, so they show nothing ([magic](magic.md#what-each-spell-does)). The ring and rays always show.

### The compass

### The party's portraits

`Portraits::Draw` (`0x8002569c`):

- **The frames**, `0x00DF` (the conversation box's) at 0.8, so 41.6 square and overlapping by 3.6, at x 84, 122, 160 and 198, all four even for an empty slot, in white at alpha 128. **The portraits** at 0.75, 30 square, 6 inside, at x 90, 128, 166 and 204, at alpha 128 too, in party order. A dead member looks the same; nothing shows hit points or who's chosen. The original never sets the portraits' blue, which carries the frames' over and is dimmed by the brightness again; at full brightness that's the same.
- **They slide** 4 pixels a frame, once a rendered frame whatever its ticks: up to 184 while they're up, down to 240, off the screen, while they're down, 14 frames either way.
- **B raises or lowers them** (`Actor::Move` at `0x80015110`), but not through a fade. **Pushing the stick past 0.05 lowers them** (`0x8001536c`), unless R holds the camera. **Standing still doesn't raise them**: only B and trek starting do (`InitGameTrek`, `0x80020f40`, which sets their flag, `0x800d813c`, and grows the minimap with it), at a new game, on loading, and after a cut-scene, a battle, a backdrop or the help menu, but not after the pause menu, a conversation or a sign. Those three are the flag's only writers. A backdrop and the help menu leave trek as a cut-scene does (their script's start sets trek's state to 0xb, `0x80051fd4`, which frees trek, and `InitGameTrek` builds it again as the script ends), so after reading a book or a plaque the portraits come up and the minimap grows, and walking off lowers them again at once; a sign shows in the box over trek (`0x80052014`) and ends without leaving it (`0x80052088`). The port starts the HUD as trek is built again (`TrekLoop.Rebuild`, `TrekHud.Start`).

### The minimap

The minimap (`MiniMap`, `minimap.cpp`) is pieces of a drawn plan, one per chunk or part of one, in eight map sets (`LoadData` `0x80049a08`, `Update` `0x8004a5a8`, `Render` `0x8004ad14`, `UpdateSection` `0x8004af0c`, `LoadSections` `0x8004b404`, `Minimap.Toggle` `0x8004b6f0`, `ShowingSpecialSection` `0x8004b96c` and `BarrowsFlags` `0x8004bfac`). The pieces and the rules are the minimap records ([hardcoded content](../hardcoded-content.md)).

- **Which map sets:** Gwernia castle (4), Ugarit 2 (14), Erromon (7), the Ehud underground (12), the Jundar interiors' mage school (11), the misc interiors' Roog entrance (6, only K03), the Barrows (9) and the Roog cave (10), loaded with the map set (`BorgMaps::LoadMap`).
- **The player's piece** is the first piece for the chunk he's in, but where a rule says otherwise. The rules: Gwernia's courtyard (I07) by floor, its ground by which side (y under 4: z above 26 or not; under 8; above); the castle walls (A01) by quarter (x under 35, z under 25); the tower (O09) and the mage school's stairs (A01) by floor; the Ehud underground's C11 by quarter (48) and I09 by x under 16; the Ehud maze room (O05) and the Roog entrance (K03) by the first of their room flags set (4222–4232, 4233–4237), or none; and in the Barrows, nine room flags (4286–4294) that show a room's other state, some another chunk's piece, and E01's strip under x 1.3 showing D01's. Positions are the player's in the chunk. The courtyard's piece `0x29` is never chosen, though it shows dim, seen from the start. A chunk with no piece shows no minimap, and the compass or nothing takes its place.
- **The box** is `0x00F5`, a square with a feathered rim, in black, stretched around the plan with a border of 8 times the scale. **Small**, with the portraits down: the plan from (237, 163) to (297, 223), the box 76 square at (229, 155), the player's piece centred, (267 − x − w/2, 193 − y − h/2) for a piece at (x, y) of width w and height h, halved by shifts. **Large**, with them up: from (177, 52) to (297, 172), the box 152 square at (161, 36), the whole plan from the corner. Over the pieces, the vignette `0x00F3` in black, stretched to the plan. The pieces are cut to the plan's area.
- **Growing and shrinking** go with the portraits (`Minimap.Toggle`, which does nothing if it's that size already), called only by B, the stick and trek starting (`Actor::Move`, `InitGameTrek`): the box's edges, the scale and the plan's place each move a 14th of the way they started from a frame, so 14 frames, linearly.
- **The group:** the pieces that show are the player's group's seen ones: a floor or a building. Another group's piece takes over at once; the old group fades out, 10 an alpha a frame, and the plan snaps to the new piece. A piece of the same group slides into place over 14 frames.
- **The colours:** the player's piece starts at grey 155 and alpha 0, rises 10 an alpha a frame to 255, its grey shimmering between 165 and 175 until its alpha is past 145, then 155. The others settle at grey 96 and alpha 128, 10 a frame. The original draws the group's pieces newest first, so the first piece of the table is on top.
- **Seeing** a piece is when it's first chosen. The courtyard's five and the tower's upper floor in Gwernia castle, and the mage school's stairs' upper floors, are seen from the start. The original saves one bit a piece, 188 in all, in the table's order (`MinimapSeen.Save`, `0x80049760`); the port saves each minimap's seen pieces ([saves](saves.md)).
- **No sounds.**

### In the port

- **On other screens,** each part keeps its place from the bottom and from its side: the sundial from the left, the compass or minimap from the right, and the portraits centred.
- **Frames:** the portraits' slide and the minimap's choice and animation step once every two ticks, the original's frame at an idealized 30 fps ([runtime](runtime.md)).
- **The fade:** the runtime's fade is a black overlay on trek's whole UI screen, so the HUD is drawn at full brightness under it, where the original dims each colour and alpha.
- **Not ported yet:** the editor showing the HUD.

## The battle screen

What a battle draws over its arena (`combatgui.cpp`, `0x80085A80`–`0x80087C60`, and the radar, `0x8008DD30`–`0x8008E3C0`), matched to the user's capture `roms/combat.png` (Brenna's turn against four rats with six in reserve) and checkpoint 02's RAM. `CombatGui` (`AidynRetold.Core.Ui`) is the model, which the battle holds (`Battle.Gui`) and steps each tick; `CombatRadar` and `CombatLogBox` are its radar and log box; `CombatGuiView` (`AidynRetold.Game`) draws it on trek's UI screen. Every part is drawn in every state of a battle, the original's in this order (`CombatGui.Draw`, `0x80085F64`; `DrawAll`, `0x800863E4`): the log box, the radar, the frames, the portraits, the HP bars.

### The portraits

`CombatGui.Create` (`0x80085B80`) makes an entry for each party member, two placeholders and each of the enemy list, reserves too, and a frame (a "bar", `CombatGui.Bars`) for each member, two spares under them and each of the list (`CreateBars`, `0x800864F4`). Their order is fixed.

- **The frames** are opaque images drawn under the portraits and tinted, so only their rims show: large `0x01C6` (28 × 26) with a 23-square portrait, small `0x01C7` (14 × 13) with an 11 × 10 one, the portraits scaled from 40. The party's run down x 270 and the enemies' x 21, from y 18, 28 apart; the party's portrait is 4 in (its HP bar's strip on the left), the enemies' 2 (the strip on the right). The first four of the list are large; the rest are small, 12 apart from y 130 (overlapping a pixel), four at x 21 and then at 35. The two spares under the party (y 18 + 28 × size) are hidden, for whoever's in neither list (a summons). The Shadow is in its battles' enemy lists, so it takes an enemy's frame. Empty frames draw nothing.
- **Who goes where** (`AssignPortrait`, `0x80085FC8`, as `Combat.CreateBody` makes each body): the first waiting entry of the combatant's entity takes its slot and the first free frame of its side, so the party in slot order top to bottom and the enemies on the field in the large frames. The first tick gives the reserves the small frames in the list's order (`0x80086C84`). A reserve that joins (`Combat.SpawnReserves`, [combat](combat.md#an-attack)) swaps frames with the first dead or fled enemy, which goes out of the fight into the small frame. Anyone in neither list takes a placeholder and the first spare.
- **They slide** (`PlaceInBar`, `0x800869F4`): every portrait starts at (160, 120), 40 square, and goes to its frame over 60 ticks of the frame's delta (`0x800D43E4`), so a second whatever the frame rate: x in a straight line, y easing out (`MenuTween.EaseOut`), the width and height easing in and out (`MenuTween.Ease`). At the start that's under the fade in.
- **The dead and fled** (`MarkDead` `0x80086208` as the body is freed, `MarkFled` `0x80086230` as a flight ends, `MarkReserveFled` `0x80086338`): the skull `0x016D` fades in over the portrait, 0 to 255 over 60 ticks, sized to it, and the portrait stays in its frame. A fled one's frame is orange (200, 164, 0, 255). They lose their HP bar and tints.
- **The HP bars** (`DrawHpBars`, `0x80087168`), over everything, at the frame at once and from the live hit points: 3 wide and the portrait's height, 4 left of the party's portrait (x 270) and 24 right of an enemy's (47), black, then the fill from the bottom, (int)(height × hp ÷ max) and never under a pixel. Its colour has no steps (`HpColour`, `0x80085A80`): u = min(255, hp × 255 ÷ max), (255 − u, u, 0).
- **The tints** are the frames' colours (`CombatGui.TintAll`, `0x8008713C`), worked out every frame, each rule over the one before: black at alpha 200; orange for a fled one; then for each in play, orange while the AI has it fleeing (`EnemyAI.IsFleeing`), dim green (0, 200, 0, 150) as set A's candidate (what the current combatant can attack), **dim red (200, 0, 0, 150) as set B's candidate**, bright green (0, 255, 0, 255) as set A's selected while an attack or a flask is aimed (states 9 and 18), and bright red (255, 0, 0, 255) as set B's selected while a spell or its area is (10 and 11) (`TintByState`, `0x80086EB4`). They don't pulse, and side doesn't matter.
- **Why red shows when nothing's being cast:** set B holds what the current combatant's *selected* spell could be cast at, and a caster keeps a selected spell (its entity's `+0x3D` and `+0x3E`) from turn to turn. So on Alaron's, Rheda's or Sholeh's turn, the dim red is on everyone their spell reaches, the party too for a healing spell, from the turn's first frame and in every state. Brenna and the rats have no spell, so their turns, the capture's among them, show none. The port shows the dim red only while a spell or its area is aimed (states 10 and 11), as Aidyn ReChronicled does (its 8e519a1), so a spell kept from an earlier turn, or cancelled, leaves no red behind: the user's choice (Oct 31, 2026).
- **C-left does nothing.** It toggles a flag (`CombatGui.Shown`, `0x800E3140`, in `Combat.ControlsByState` at `0x80085114`) that nothing reads, in 1.0 or the debug build, so nothing hides the screen. The port has no flag.

### The log box

A `TextBand` (`CombatGui.LogBoxCreate`, `0x80087A78`), the cinematics' caption box's widget ([cinematics](cinematics.md#the-caption-box)), not the conversation box's, that never drops away. `CombatLogBox` lays it out.

- **The original's** rectangle is (0, 180) to (246, 240), with no growing: the band from 180 down and the gradient 11 tall above it, both right across the frame; the line in the main font in (225, 225, 225, 200) at (76, 190), wrapping at 241 and cut at 230, which keeps it off the radar; the conversation box's border `0x00DF` at (20, 174) and the current combatant's portrait at (26, 180), level with the band's top. An enemy's turn shows the enemy's portrait. Before the first turn's line it shows only the band.
- **The port floats it as the conversation box**, the user's choice, so the two read as one panel: laid out in the original's frame at the bottom centre of the UI screen, the band from 16 to 304 and 180 to 228, feathered on every side with the conversation box's gradients (10 above, 12 below, 16 beside), and the border and portrait at (20, 180) and (26, 186), level with the box's top as the conversation box has them, 6 lower than the original's. The line stays at the original's (76, 190), lowered as the box rarely holds more than one.
- **The lines** are the battle's (`CombatLog`, `Battle.Lines`, the struct's `+0x4FC4`–`+0x52C4`): shown, queued and a third. `Show` replaces the line at once (`Combat.ShowLog`, `0x8005E604`); `Queue` only keeps the next (`QueueLog`, `0x8005E658`), and `Flush` shows it at a state's change (`FlushQueuedLog`, `0x8005E6F4`: an attack's result as its animation ends, a cast's end, a potion), or the third in its place if Banish queued one (`QueueLog2`). So "Brenna attacks Giant Rat" shows at once, and "Giant Rat killed" queued behind it comes up as the blow lands. **There's no timer**: a line holds until the next. `CombatLog` is the box's one writer, and cues each change to the battle's host ([combat](combat.md#what-a-battle-tells-its-host)).
- **The hold** (`+0x543C`, `Combat.HoldForMessage`, `0x80022AEC`): only a reserve fleeing as it arrives sets it, as the next turn begins ([combat](combat.md#the-ai)). For 60 ticks the box shows "Enemy reinforcements flee" and the battle's states and input wait; then the shown line comes back.
- **Locked** as the party falls (`TurnOrder.Defeat`, `0x8006A780`): its line, and in a non-lethal battle Alaron's portrait, and nothing changes the box after (`CombatLog.Lock`).

### The radar

`CombatRadar.Draw` (`0x8008DF04`):

- **The ring** is the sundial's, `0x001B`, at (245, 170), white at alpha 164 (the HUD's are 80), under the dots.
- **The dots** are 2 × 2, alpha 164, one for each body in use, by slot: the camera's owner yellow (150, 150, 0) at (274, 199), the party green (0, 150, 0) and the rest red (150, 0, 0), under the frames.
- **It's centred on the camera's owner** (`0x800D8448`): the current combatant, or while an attack is aimed the target cursor's body (`Battle.CameraOwner`). A body gone from the field has no dot. **It turns with the camera**: with (a, b) the camera's look in X and Z (`rotationXZ`), a body (dx, dz) from the owner goes (dx × −b + dz × a) × 0.5 right and (dx × a + dz × b) × 0.5 up, truncated, so the way the camera looks is up. Half a pixel a unit. A body more than 20 pixels out is held at 20, its colour halved.
- **While a spell, its area or a flask is aimed** (states 10, 11 and 18), the dots of set B's selected throb, each channel that isn't 0 plus the pulse, and the rest are halved. The pulse (`CombatRadar.Step`, `0x8008DE44`) goes from 0 to −150 and back, 4 a frame, 76 frames a cycle. A flask aims with set A, which the radar doesn't read, so in state 18 every dot is halved.
- **Checkpoint 02** (Rheda's turn) pins it: every dot where the original's arithmetic puts it (`BattleCheckpointTests`).

### On other screens

The user chose where the screen sits on a screen of another shape (`CombatGui.Draw`), and it follows the Menu size setting, as the HUD does:

- **The enemies' column** keeps its place from the screen's top left corner, and **the party's** from the top right.
- **The log box** floats at the bottom centre.
- **The radar** sits in the screen's bottom right corner, the user's choice, its place in the frame kept from that corner as the compass's is, so on a wide screen it's clear of the log box; or, as the original has it, in the log box at its place in the frame (`RadarAnchor`). On a 4:3 screen the two are the same. F9 swaps them, and `--radar box` starts in the box.

### In the port

- **Ticks:** the portraits' tweens step a tick at a time (the original's delta), and the radar's pulse once a frame at 30 fps.
- **The fade** darkens the whole UI screen, as for the HUD; the original fades each widget's colour but not the HP bars' black.
- **The target sets** are the battle's ([combat](combat.md#an-attack)), which the tints and the radar read through `Battle.Marks`, with the AI's fleeing flag (`Combatant.Fleeing`).
- **The aim's marker** (`Combat.DrawTargetCounts`, `0x80021448`) isn't the GUI's and draws no numbers: it's a cylinder in the arena ([combat](combat.md#spells-potions-and-skills-in-a-turn)), the runtime's `AimMarkerView`.

### The held menus

C-down's spells and skills and C-up's potions and item spells (`CombatMenu`, states 22 and 23), open while their button is held and chosen from as it's let go; [combat](combat.md#spells-potions-and-skills-in-a-turn) has what they list and do. `Ui.CombatMenu` is the model the battle holds (`Battle.Menu`), and `CombatGuiView.DrawMenu` draws it over the battle screen at the original frame's centre. Matched to the user's captures of Alaron's turn in the rats' battle, `roms/combat-cdown.png` (Perform, Air Shield, Spirit Shield, Wind and three `vs.` spells, with `vs. Star` below the list) and `roms/combat-cup.png` (four potions and Whitefire).

- **The list** is a `ChoicePopup` (`ChoicePopup.CreateList`, `0x80045AF4`, with 120 and 95; 55 without the Expansion Pak, and the port has the Pak's): a `WidgetPulseMenu` 120 wide at x 99, cut to x 99–221 and its top to its bottom, inclusive (`Widget.SetClipRect`, `0x80004C38`). `ChoicePopup.Layout` (`0x80046488`) makes it as tall as its rows up to 95 and puts its top at 120 less half that, so 73 for a full list.
- **The panel** is the conversation box's feathered black one (`ChoicePopup.CreatePanel`, `0x80045F24`: `0x01C4` drawn black, edges `0x01C0`–`0x01C3`, corners `0x01BC`–`0x01BF`), each piece an inclusive rectangle (`Widget.CreateImageBoxItem`, `0x800B310C`): the centre 100 to 220 across, the list's height down, a pixel right of the list's left edge; the top and bottom edges a pixel taller than their images, the sides a pixel wider and a pixel further out. No title, no padding.
- **A row** (`CombatMenu.MakeEntry`, `0x80088714`) is its icon at the list's left edge and its text in the main font after it, at the menu's icon width + 5 (10, the lute's, for C-down; 16, the flask's, for C-up, whatever the row's own icon is: so x 114 and 120) and half that width less half the text's 11 down (0 and 3). A row is as tall as its icon or its text, 11 for C-down's and 16 for C-up's, and **3 more under it** (`Widget.SetSpacing`, `0x800B2098`, which both builders call on the list before laying it out; `WidgetPulseMenu.Step`, `0x80027C98`, adds it after every row, the last too), so C-down's rows are 14 apart and C-up's 19. Five of C-up's fill the 95 exactly. A spell with no school (the `vs.` spells) has no icon and its text keeps its place. The icons are drawn as they are; the text is the row's colour: skills blue (0, 128, 225, 225), spells red (225, 80, 80, 225), potions green (0, 200, 0, 225), item spells yellow (200, 200, 0, 225).
- **The cursor** has no box: its row's colour goes from (100, 100, 100, 100) toward its own and back, 20 ticks each way (`WidgetPulseMenu.Draw`, `0x8002809C`; `Color.Mix`, `0x80027F1C`, truncating each channel), its icon unchanged. It wraps; the stick's or D-pad's up and down move it.
- **Scrolling**: the list follows the cursor 10 pixels a tick, until the cursor's row, its gap left out, is inside it.
- **The arrows** `0x38D0` and `0x38CF` (5 × 8, white) are placed as the menu is built, 8 left of the list (x 91), the up arrow its height and 2 above the list's top and the down arrow 2 below its bottom (y 170 for a full list). `ScrollArrows` (`Create` `0x800B1640`, `Step` `0x800B1908`) fades each from 0 as the menu opens, 20 a tick to 255 and back: the up arrow while the first row is above the list's top, the down arrow while the last row's foot is below its bottom, so a list that just fits shows none.
- **Nothing to list** shows the Party tab's message instead: "No spells or skills available" or "No potions or item spells available".
- **Sense Aura's popup** is that message box too, its lines white and centred, as wide as its longest up to seven pixels a character (`CombatGuiView.DrawAura`).
- **Checked against the captures** (`--ui-screenshot` of the same turn from a save of the Combat 01 checkpoint, compared on the capture's even pixels, which are the console's own): every row's text, every icon and the down arrow line up at the same pixel, with the residual within the dither (an RMS of 7 to 20 a channel, under the log box's line's 33, which the battle screen was matched by), and the text runs to the same columns. The held stick repeats as the menus' do.

## A faithful mode, later

The floating panels and the resizable screen are enhancements the user chose over the original's look, leaving a faithful mode for a setting later (ADR 0018, [roadmap](../roadmap.md) Phase 6). It would fix `UiScreen` at 320 × 240, pillarboxed at 4:3 with the 3D view, and draw [the overscan bars](#the-screen) over both last. The conversation box and the battle's log box would take the original's bands right across the frame ([above](#the-conversation-box), [the log box](#the-log-box)): a `FeatheredPanel` with no bottom or side feathering, which `UiImages.DrawPanel` already skips. The rest of the UI is already at its original place. The Expansion Pak's high resolution would draw the 320-wide canvas into a 512-wide one, bilinear, before the VideoOut filter, with bars of 20 at the sides; the VI's filters and a TV's look would be further `UiFilter`s.

## In the editor

The editor shows the UI over a plain backdrop on the pages that use it: a script's player the conversation box, a loot table's roll and a container on the map the loot window, a shop's page its window, a trainer's character page the training window, and the inventory record's page the Party tab, whose buttons act on the editor's game. The UI screen passes the mouse to its views in its own pixels (`ForwardsMouse`), and hovering a choice or row moves the cursor. `UiScreenOptions` holds the zoom, aspect, lines and filter; the command-line flags for screenshots are in [the editor's command line](editor.md#the-command-line).
